3 things we might be wrong to wait on

These got parked, but the reason doesn't hold up. Read them and tell me if you agree they should move to "build now."

1 · Get your 5 projects healthy and in sync the big miss

What it isRight now each of your 5 projects has its own copy of the robot's rulebook, and they've quietly drifted into 5 different versions. This is: put them all on one shared rulebook (so a fix in one reaches all five), lock the 15 tools you borrowed from someone else's project to a version you've checked (so they can't change under you without warning), and add a small check that catches when the rulebook points at a file that doesn't exist (yours does this today — a real, current bug).
Why we parked itThe audit said "you only have 1 project, so syncing projects is a problem you don't have yet."
Why that's wrongYou have five. The problem isn't in the future — it's here now, getting worse every week as they drift apart. (This is the exact hole you predicted last time: "we keep finding things that got hidden by bad assumptions.")
Where it came fromYour own correction last session, plus the research on running an AI helper across many projects.
My pickPull it forward. The cheap part (one shared rulebook + locking the borrowed tools + the broken-link check) is build-now. The fancy "auto-push a change to all 5 at once" can still wait.

2 · Stress-test the money math core risk

What it isAdd a tool that throws hundreds of weird number combinations at the pricing math and checks the total is never wrong — never negative, never taxes the wrong thing, never off by a penny. Normal tests only check the few cases a person thought of; this checks the ones nobody thought of.
Why we parked itIt needs one small extra library, and "needs a library" got it filed under "decide later."
Why that's wrongA wrong total on a $30k quote is the single worst thing this product can do — and it has nothing to do with the robot working on its own. It's a risk today. The only thing blocking it is you saying "yes, add the library." Even the "where can we cut?" pass marked this one "do NOT cut."
Where it came fromThe research on AI tools that quietly weaken their own tests over time — money math is where that hurts most.
My pickPull it forward. The library is a well-known, widely-used one; I'll show you the exact safety stats (how popular, last updated) before we actually install anything.

3 · The easy accessibility win nearly free

What it isAdd a rule that makes the buttons and fields in your app easy to find by name. The exact same thing that lets the robot reliably click the right button also makes your app work for people using screen readers. Today your app has none of these name-tags, so any robot click is a guess.
Why we parked itIts main payoff (the robot checking screens by itself) waits until the robot does visual work on its own — which is later.
Why that's worth a lookIt's nearly free — it's an automatic rule that runs when code is saved — and it helps real human users right now, today, regardless of the robot.
Where it came fromThe research on letting an AI safely test a real web page.
My pickPull it forward — it's a tiny, low-risk yes that pays off both for users and for later robot work.
