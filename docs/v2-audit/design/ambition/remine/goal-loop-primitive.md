# Ambition Re-mine — /goal: The Loop Primitive vs. the Harness Pipeline

## What this source is (plain English, 2-3 sentences)

A Notion research article analyzing Claude Code's `/goal` primitive — a session-scoped slash command that turns a turn-at-a-time agent session into a self-continuing loop that keeps starting new turns until a user-authored stopping condition becomes true (e.g. "all tests in test/auth pass and lint is clean"). The article's mechanical core: after each turn a separate fresh small model (Haiku) reads the transcript and judges "are we done?" — the working agent is not its own grader — but that grader runs no tools, so it judges only what Claude surfaced into the conversation. The deeper contribution the article half-states is a **taxonomy of verifiers ranked by distance from ground truth**, which gives a reusable test for any "autonomous stop" mechanism: does the stop signal read the transcript, or does it read the ground truth?

## The full-ambition moves this source teaches

### Move: Adopt `/goal` as the per-task continuation loop primitive (the autonomous "keep going until done")

- **Plain-English what & why:** Right now a Claude Code session stops and waits for a human "continue" after every turn. For a well-specified task ("make the auth tests pass and lint clean"), that human-in-the-loop tap is pure friction — the agent could keep going on its own until the finish line is actually crossed. `/goal` is exactly that: you write a verifiable end-state, and the agent self-continues turn after turn until a fresh grader model confirms the state is true. The failure it prevents is the operator babysitting a session that has a clean, checkable finish line — and the *worse* failure it prevents versus a naive "just loop forever" hack is that the stop decision is made by a *separate* model reading the work, not by the working agent rationalizing its own success.
- **The mechanism:** Install `/goal` as a first-class harness primitive (Claude Code v2.1.139+ ships it; it's a wrapper around a session-scoped prompt-based Stop hook). Wire it as the continuation driver for a single, well-specified, reversible `/queue` task — the "one-shot routine for well-specified work" the article maps to Node 13.3. Concretely: a `/queue` task picks up a spec, runs the work, and instead of a hand-written Stop hook or a human tap, `/goal` drives continuation with a condition pinned to *structural* gates. The harness contributes the standing rule that the condition must bottom out on real verifiers (MUST-FIX=0, CI green, diff under cap) plus a turn/time bound, never a prose claim like "the feature works."
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence — `/goal` is registered nowhere on disk. The runtime skill list registers `/loop` but no `/goal`; the map's skill inventory `[map §3b]` lists 26 disk skills and the canon set, and `/goal` appears in neither (pass3 (b)(3)). This is a build-or-reject decision, not a drift to reconcile.
- **Conservative disposition:** §C deferred — and deferred hard. Pass3's net recommendation: "Treat `/goal` as a deferred, low-priority continuation wrapper whose adoption is *blocked* until the two structural prerequisites... are built — and even then, scope it to direct-`/queue` reversible tasks only." The conservative synthesis treated `/goal` as a low-priority convenience gated behind two internal fixes.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE.** The dismissal fails the new charter on two counts. First, "low-priority convenience" is wrong: `/goal` is the **loop primitive** — the in-session continuation engine that autonomous work requires. The new charter names "self-improving loops" and "bug → reviewed PR" as first-class. You cannot run a bug-to-PR loop, or a self-improving loop, without a primitive that decides "should there be another turn?" against a verifiable end-state. `/goal` IS that primitive; deferring it defers autonomy itself. Second, the pass3 framing inverted cause and effect: it said "adoption is *blocked* until `.cr-ok`-reaches-CI and `/cr` REJECT routing exist." Under the new charter that's backwards — those two prerequisites are themselves IN-SCOPE-NOW autonomy work (the unforgeable merge gate and the REJECT path are load-bearing for *any* autonomous loop, not just `/goal`). So rather than `/goal` being blocked by them, `/goal` is the forcing function that makes them mandatory. Elevate from "deferred low-priority" to "in-scope-now, co-sequenced with the two structural prerequisites it depends on." The old reasoning was conservative bias of the "solve everything else first, the autonomy primitive can wait" variety — exactly the deferral the new charter retires.

### Move: Build the verifier-rung taxonomy as the harness's standing test for every autonomous stop signal

- **Plain-English what & why:** The most durable idea in the article is never stated as its thesis. It implicitly ranks four ways an agent can decide "I'm done," by how far each sits from ground truth: (1) the working agent's own confidence — weakest (Codex's "fairly confident"); (2) a fresh model that reads only the transcript and runs no tools — Claude `/goal`'s Haiku grader, "a reader of one agent's homework"; (3) a fresh-context reviewer that actually reads the diff and runs tools — `/cr`; (4) a CI required-check bound to a sentinel, enforced outside the loop at merge — the only truly unforgeable gate. The failure this taxonomy prevents is **trust-laundering**: an operator wiring up a rung-2 stop signal (transcript grader) but extending it rung-4 trust (treating its "done" as ship-permission). Every future "autonomous stop" feature — and there will be many under an autonomy-first charter — needs to be classified before it's trusted.
- **The mechanism:** Promote the rung taxonomy from buried analysis to an explicit, documented harness doctrine (a section in the autonomy/verification design doc, referenced from CLAUDE.md). The standing rule it generates: *any stop signal that decides whether autonomous work ships must bottom out at rung 3 or rung 4; a rung-1 or rung-2 signal may control continuation but never certification.* This becomes the litmus test applied to `/goal`, to cloud `/schedule` routines, to any self-improving loop, and to any future Anthropic primitive. It also names the real test for `/goal` vs `/loop` selection (pass2 §6): use `/goal` only when the condition becomes true as a *direct causal result of the agent's own in-session actions* — "tests pass" qualifies, "deploy is green" does not (the latter spins forever and belongs to `/loop` or `/schedule`).
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence — no verifier-classification doctrine exists in the harness. The map records the canon is "internally inconsistent" with `/cr` pass-counts described three ways `[map §3c]` and no single canonical `/cr`-verdict artifact `[map §3f]`; there is no rung framework anywhere. (pass2 §1, pass3 (c).)
- **Conservative disposition:** §D minor / summarized-away. The conservative synthesis treated the taxonomy as analytical color supporting the "defer `/goal`" conclusion rather than as a deliverable in its own right. It never proposed building or documenting the rung framework.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: NEW.** The conservative synthesis summarized this away entirely — it used the taxonomy to justify caution about `/goal` and then dropped it. Recover it as a first-class candidate. Under an autonomy-first charter where multiple stop-signal mechanisms will proliferate (`/goal`, cloud routines, heartbeats, self-improving loops), a *shared classifier* for "can this signal certify, or only continue?" is exactly the kind of clarity-over-minimalism artifact the new charter wants — it earns its place because its job is obvious and it prevents a concrete, recurring failure (trust-laundering a transcript grader into a ship gate). This is the highest-leverage thing the source teaches and the conservative read buried it.

### Move: Close the unforgeable-gate hole — wire `.cr-ok` verification into CI (autonomy's load-bearing prerequisite)

- **Plain-English what & why:** The article assumes the harness already has a truly unforgeable gate: a CI required-check that re-verifies the `.cr-ok` sentinel at merge, so no amount of an agent *claiming* "review passed" can substitute for the gate actually having run. The map says this is false on our disk: `.cr-ok` is gitignored and consumed only by the local pre-push hook — **CI never verifies it** (the Node 8.5(c) hole). The failure this prevents is the most dangerous one in autonomous operation: an agent loop concludes "done" on a forged or merely-narrated success, and because nothing re-checks ground truth at merge, the false-positive ships. This is cheap to run and expensive to discover — it surfaces downstream as a merged bad PR or comprehension debt weeks later (pass2 §5).
- **The mechanism:** Make CI the authoritative re-check. Either have CI re-run the verification that `.cr-ok` claims to represent, or stop trusting a local sentinel for ship-permission and put the real gate (review + tests + build) inside the required CI check that runs on every PR. The point is that the gate enforced *outside the loop, at merge,* is the one no transcript narration can forge. This is the rung-4 backstop the entire autonomy story depends on — without it, every autonomous loop's stop signal is the *last* check, not a layer beneath a strong one.
- **Citation:** Disk fact — "The Node 8.5(c) gap (CI never verifies `.cr-ok`) is a disk fact `[HARNESS-AS-IS §8]`; canon's `.cr-ok` chain has the same hole (gitignored, never reaches CI)" `[map §3f, lines 229-231]`. (pass3 (b)(2).)
- **Conservative disposition:** §C deferred, but correctly flagged as the single most important finding — "the decisive gap... `.cr-ok` never reaches CI." Pass3 named it as an internal engineering prerequisite that *blocks* `/goal`. The conservative synthesis kept it as a prerequisite-to-something-deferred rather than promoting it to its own priority.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE.** Pass3 was right that this is the decisive gap but wrong about its priority class. It framed it as a precondition for a deferred convenience. Under the new charter this is **the keystone of safe autonomy** and should be top-of-list IN-SCOPE-NOW on its own merits — independent of `/goal`. The charter says "disable-model-invocation + an unforgeable gate is what makes autonomy safe." We have the first half (side-effect skills can be hidden from the model) but the map proves the second half is a hole. Every autonomous mechanism the new charter wants — bug→PR, Slack summon, scheduled cloud routines, self-improving loops — ships its output through merge. If merge isn't a real rung-4 gate, autonomy is unsafe by construction. The old reasoning's bias: it treated the unforgeable gate as a `/goal` dependency rather than as the foundation the whole autonomy charter rests on. Elevate from "prerequisite for a deferred wrapper" to "P0 autonomy-enablement work."

### Move: Build the REJECT / UNATTENDED fallback path for autonomous loops

- **Plain-English what & why:** An autonomous loop that *can't* reach its finish line must not spin forever or silently give up — it must hand off cleanly. The article's prescription: a `/goal` run that can't reach its condition falls back to existing routing — when running unattended (UNATTENDED=1), re-queue the task for a spec/change pass; when interactive, surface as NEEDS HUMAN. The goal loop ends; the harness takes over. The failure this prevents is the runaway: an autonomous session burning turns (and tokens) against an unreachable condition with no escape hatch and no human signal — or worse, quietly stopping with the work half-done and no one notified.
- **The mechanism:** Build the REJECT tier into `/cr` and the loop-exit routing: (a) a bounded loop (turn/time cap baked into every autonomous condition); (b) on cap-exceeded-without-success, branch on UNATTENDED — re-queue to a `/change`-style spec path if unattended, emit NEEDS HUMAN (and, under the new charter, a Slack/push notification) if interactive. This is the session-level analogue of the 5-PR overnight cap, and it's the safety rail that makes "summon from Slack, get a reviewed PR or a clear NEEDS-HUMAN back" actually work.
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence — `/cr`'s structure on disk has "**No REJECT tier, no UNATTENDED branching**" `[map §3c, line 182]`; no `/change` skill is listed among the 26 disk skills `[map §3b]`. (pass3 (b)(4).)
- **Conservative disposition:** §C deferred — bundled with the `.cr-ok` fix as the second internal prerequisite that "blocks" `/goal`. Treated as engineering plumbing behind a deferred convenience.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE.** The REJECT/UNATTENDED branch is not plumbing for a convenience — it is the **failure-mode contract for every autonomous trigger** the new charter elevates. A bug→PR loop summoned from Slack/Linear *needs* a defined answer to "what happens when the agent can't finish?" — silence is not an option when no human is watching. The old reasoning scoped it as a `/goal` dependency; under autonomy-first it's a shared requirement of the entire trigger front-door. Elevate from "prerequisite for deferred `/goal`" to "in-scope-now, part of the core autonomous-loop exit contract." Co-sequence with the `.cr-ok`/CI fix above — together they are the safety floor that lets every autonomous loop run unattended.

## Autonomy angle

This source is, under its surface, **the most autonomy-relevant of the verification sources** — even though it reads as a cautious "should we adopt this tool" review.

- **Self-improving / in-session loops:** `/goal` is the literal continuation primitive — "should there be another turn?" decided against a verifiable end-state. Any self-improving loop or bug→PR loop needs exactly this engine. The article teaches both the primitive and the guardrails (structural stop condition, turn cap, reversibility gating).
- **Cloud scheduling:** The article distinguishes `/goal` (producer-loop, closed by the agent's own in-session work) from `/loop` (poller, closed by the world — time, an external deploy). Under the resolved fact that cloud `/schedule` exists on Anthropic infra, this distinction is the selection rule for *which* loop primitive a scheduled routine should use: `/goal` for "finish this task," `/loop`/`/schedule` for "watch the world." A scheduled routine that needs to *complete work* (not just poll) is a `/goal` run inside a cloud routine.
- **The safety preconditions for autonomy:** The two gaps the article exposes — CI never re-verifies `.cr-ok` (no rung-4 backstop at merge) and `/cr` has no REJECT/UNATTENDED branch — are precisely the load-bearing prerequisites for *any* unattended trigger (Slack/Linear summon → reviewed PR). The article's real autonomy lesson: you cannot safely run autonomous loops until the merge gate is unforgeable and the can't-finish path is defined.

Net: this source teaches the loop primitive, the rule for when to use it, the verifier-rung test that keeps its grader from being mistaken for a ship gate, and the two structural fixes that make unattended operation safe.

## The single biggest move (one paragraph)

**Adopt `/goal` as the autonomous continuation primitive — and co-sequence the two structural fixes it forces (CI-verified `.cr-ok`; `/cr` REJECT/UNATTENDED routing) as P0 autonomy-enablement, not as blockers.** The conservative synthesis inverted the priority: it treated `/goal` as a low-value convenience to defer until two "prerequisites" were built. Under the autonomy-first charter the truth is the reverse — `/goal` is the loop engine that *every* self-improving or bug→PR flow needs, and the so-called prerequisites (an unforgeable merge gate, a defined can't-finish exit) are the foundation of all unattended operation, not obstacles to one wrapper. This is world-class because the source doesn't just hand us a primitive; it hands us the **rung taxonomy** that tells us exactly where that primitive's grader sits (rung 2, transcript-bound) and exactly what it must bottom out on (rung 4, CI at merge) before any autonomous loop's "done" can be trusted to ship. Pick up `/goal` first, build it on top of a now-unforgeable gate and a now-defined REJECT path, and document the rung taxonomy as the standing test for every future autonomous stop signal.
