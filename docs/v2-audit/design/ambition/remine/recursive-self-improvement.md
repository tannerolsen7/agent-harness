# Ambition Re-mine — "Recursive Self-Improvement: Verification Is the Scarce Capability, and Yours Is Uncalibrated" (2026)

## What this source is (plain English, 2-3 sentences)

A 2026 essay reacting to Anthropic Institute's "When AI Builds Itself" that strips the recursive-self-improvement (RSI) politics down to one engineering claim: as code authorship gets automated, **verification — not generation — becomes the scarce, load-bearing capability**, and a self-improving loop graded by the same model family optimizes toward that model's *preference*, not toward truth. Pass 2 sharpens the thesis into a structural one: every quality gate in an automated loop degenerates into a measure of the model's self-agreement unless its terminal authority is anchored to something the model cannot redefine — Pass 2 names this disease **authority laundering**. The deepest move is that the truly scarce resource is not "verification" but a **human-authored, model-immutable specification of what correct means** (CI, property tests, golden sets are all just enforcement machinery for a spec; none of them *produce* the spec).

## The full-ambition moves this source teaches

### Move: Unforgeable terminal stop authority (CI-gated convergence, enforced where the loop can't reach it)

- **Plain-English what & why:** Today, when the autonomous loop decides it is "done," it reaches that conclusion by *its own model passes agreeing that there are zero MUST-FIX items*, then writes the `.cr-ok` sentinel and pushes. The thing being graded computes its own passing grade. The failure this prevents: a loop can converge — write the sentinel, push, eventually merge — purely because the reviewer agents share the generator's blind spots and framing. No external oracle ever confirmed the code actually passes the real tests on the exact SHA being shipped. The fix is to make the *stop condition* require both "model reviewers found zero MUST-FIX" **AND** "the CI required-checks are green on the sentinel'd SHA," and to enforce that conjunction in a place the loop physically cannot forge — branch protection / a CI job that validates the sentinel — not inside the skill body the model controls.
- **The mechanism:** (1) A CI job (`verify-cr-ok.yml` or a step in `ci.yml`) that reads the `.cr-ok` sentinel, parses its `branch:sha`, and **fails the check unless the sentinel SHA equals the head SHA AND all required checks are green**. (2) Branch protection on `main` makes that job a required check, so a push whose sentinel is stale, missing, or pointing at a non-green SHA cannot merge. (3) Rename the conceptual gate from "cross-MODEL review" to "cross-AUTHORITY review" in the `/cr` doctrine so the distinction (model agreement ≠ oracle pass) is named, not implicit. This closes the **Node 8.5(c) hole** directly: the sentinel currently is gitignored and never reaches CI.
- **Citation:** [CANONICAL §3f, lines 229-231] — "The Node 8.5(c) gap (CI never verifies `.cr-ok`) is a disk fact … canon's `.cr-ok` chain has the same hole (gitignored, never reaches CI)." Combined with [CANONICAL §3c] — `/cr`'s MUST-FIX tiers are *model-computed*. The pieces (`.cr-ok` sentinel chain [§3f, §5], hard CI oracle `ci.yml`+`integration.yml` [§3f]) physically exist; only the wiring between them is absent.
- **Conservative disposition:** §C deferred / partially in MASTER-FINDINGS's "6 wiring-tweak MOVES." Pass 3 logged it as gap R-1 and called the fix an "internal wiring decision … no external research needed," which reads as low-priority housekeeping. It is treated as one tweak among six, with Pass 2's qualifier ("less forgeable, not unforgeable — coverage-bounded oracle") used to *soften* the urgency.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE.** This is the single most charter-critical move in the entire source and the conservative framing badly under-weights it. Under the OLD charter (human frames/reviews/merges every change) a forgeable stop gate is a quality annoyance — the human catches it. Under the NEW charter **autonomy is first-class**: bug→reviewed-PR, summoned from Slack/Linear, self-improving loops, cloud `/schedule` running unattended while the laptop is closed. The instant a human is no longer in the merge path, the forgeable convergence gate becomes the **load-bearing safety boundary of the entire autonomous system** — it is the *only* thing standing between "model agreed with itself" and "shipped to main." This is not a wiring tweak; it is the foundational invariant that makes every other autonomy feature safe to turn on. It should be **in-scope-now, P0, and a hard prerequisite gate for enabling any unattended `/queue` or cloud-scheduled push.** Pass 2's "coverage-bounded, not unforgeable" caveat is *true but is an argument for ELEVATE, not against* — it means you must ALSO build R-2 (measure the coverage), not that R-1 is optional.

### Move: Continuous reviewer-recall calibration (the golden set, as a CI concern not a one-time act)

- **Plain-English what & why:** You cannot honestly claim "verification is our strongest capability" while the verifier's recall is literally zero-knowledge — nobody knows whether `/cr` catches 1-in-5 defects or 4-in-5. The failure this prevents: shipping an autonomous loop whose only quality gate has an *unknown and possibly catastrophic* miss rate, and discovering it in production against a paying stranger. The fix is a **golden set**: a curated corpus of known-defective diffs each paired with the known-correct findings, run against `/cr` to measure recall (what fraction of seeded defects it catches). Pass 2's sharpening is the world-class part: recall is not a fixed property of `/cr`, it is a property of (the passes) × (the merge rule) × (the model version) × (the diff distribution) — so a number measured once on Opus 4.8 is stale the moment any of those change. Therefore the golden set must be **re-run on every change to the reviewer**, exactly like a test suite — calibration is a continuous-integration concern, not a one-time blessing.
- **The mechanism:** (1) A `golden-set/` corpus of seeded-defect diffs + expected-findings keys, seeded with **adversarial/known-defective** cases (not just historical friendly diffs — see R-4). (2) A harness command (`/cr-calibrate` or a CI job) that runs `/cr` over the corpus and emits a recall number + per-defect-class breakdown. (3) A freshness/ownership rule: recall is re-measured on any change to `/cr` passes, the tier-merge rule, or the model version, and a stale recall number blocks promotion of the reviewer as a trusted gate. (4) Doctrine: a calibrated `/cr` is a **triage** layer that sizes the human/CI net beneath it — never a *terminal* authority. Calibration tells you the leakage rate; it does not make leakage zero.
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence — Pass 3 R-2: grep across the entire `docs/research/v2-audit` corpus + both ground-truth files finds **no** `/cr` catch-rate, recall, or defect-detection measurement; "golden" appears only as the unrelated "Golden rule" [CANONICAL §9] and one "golden-exemplar reads" mention. The freshness gap is itself cited: [CANONICAL §4] documents freshness rules exist for only 3 of ~14 knowledge stores and read-time for ~5 of 14.
- **Conservative disposition:** §C deferred, though Pass 3 calls it (echoing the article) "the single most overdue item." MASTER-FINDINGS keeps it as a deferred/hypothesis-gated build item rather than a now-move; Pass 3 hedged it as "a build-plan item, not an as-is-map row" and noted siblings (`coderabbit`, `stripe-minions-kaliski`) already touch catch-rate, implying "synthesize, don't prioritize."
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE.** The conservative read deferred this because under the old charter an uncalibrated reviewer is backstopped by a human reading every diff. Under the new charter the reviewer **is** the backstop — there is no human in the unattended loop — so its miss rate is the system's actual defect-escape rate, and shipping autonomy on an unmeasured gate is shipping blind. This should be **in-scope-now and a hard gate on unattended autonomy**: cap `/queue`/cloud-`/schedule` self-merge until recall is measured and above a stated floor. The world-class version is bigger than "build a golden set once" — it is a **standing calibration suite that runs in CI on every reviewer/model change**, with an owner and a freshness rule (folding in R-5). That is CodeRabbit/Augment-grade verification rigor, and we currently have *zero* of it. The "siblings already discuss catch-rate" dismissal is conservative bias: discussing a concept in a research pass is not the same as having a running calibration harness.

### Move: Property-based testing on money math (a human-authored, model-immutable correctness spec)

- **Plain-English what & why:** Example-based tests check the cases a developer (or a model) happened to think of — and when the model writes both the implementation and the tests, it tends to test exactly the inputs the implementation already handles. Property-based testing (PBT) instead asserts an *invariant* — a rule that must hold for **all** inputs — and the framework generates hundreds of adversarial inputs trying to break it. The failure this prevents: a pricing bug that survives because nobody (human or model) wrote the example that exposes it. This product is the textbook case: a proposal/pricing tool with integer-cents money, line-item totals, tax-on-goods-only, discounts, and service fees, where the invariants are crisp and a wrong total is a $30k-client-facing disaster. The deep reason this is the *highest-value* lever (Pass 2's insight): a property is "something the model can't argue with" — it is a **human-authored, model-immutable specification of correctness**, which Pass 2 identifies as the actual scarce resource the whole essay is circling. PBT doesn't just test; it *encodes the spec the loop cannot forge*.
- **The mechanism:** Adopt a PBT library (e.g. `fast-check`, vetted against the harness dependency rule — name/purpose/weekly-downloads/last-publish/ships-its-own-types — and confirmed to integrate with Vitest 4 / TS 5). Define the minimal invariant set for money math: `total = sum(line items)`; tax never applied to service fees; no negative line totals; discounts never produce negative subtotals; integer-cents round-tripping is exact. Seed a `learned-patterns.md` / PITFALLS constraint: **any change to pricing/total logic requires a property test**, enforced as a coverage-style blocker for the pricing module.
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence — Pass 3 R-3: grep for `property-based|property test|fast-check|PBT` across the v2-audit corpus AND the ground-truth files returns **nothing**. As-is testing [CLAUDE.md → Testing; CANONICAL §3f] is example-based Vitest + real-DB integration only.
- **Conservative disposition:** §C deferred, with the one *narrow fresh-research warrant* in Pass 3 (vet `fast-check` for Vitest 4). MASTER-FINDINGS treats it as a deferred "property-based testing for money-math" item, gated on tooling vetting.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE (and it is the de-risking core of autonomy).** This is the rare deferral that was *under*-sold even on its own merits, independent of scale. PBT is the cheapest, most durable way to put a *model-immutable* correctness anchor under the exact logic where a silent error is most expensive. Under autonomy it becomes structurally essential: when the loop writes both the pricing code AND its example tests (Pass 2's "the oracle is increasingly model-authored" crack), the human-specified invariant is the *only* check the loop cannot quietly weaken. The "deferred pending tooling vetting" framing is fine as a 30-minute step, not as a reason to schedule it late — the vetting is trivial and the payoff is the system's strongest unforgeable gate. Make it **in-scope-now**, immediately after R-1/R-2, and the *first* place the harness demonstrates "human-authored spec the model can't redefine."

### Move: Adversarial / paying-stranger signal seeds the calibration (kill the warm-audience bias)

- **Plain-English what & why:** Every verification claim in this harness has been validated against Monica and family — a warm, friendly audience that never feeds the system adversarial input. The cited research (16.6% AI-suggestion adoption, >50% of un-adopted suggestions wrong) lands hardest *because the system has never met a paying stranger*. The failure this prevents: a golden set built only from historical friendly diffs will *underestimate* the reviewer's real-world leakage, because friendly diffs don't contain the adversarial defects strangers' usage surfaces. So adversarial signal is not a separate gate — it is a **required input to the calibration corpus**: seed the golden set with known-defective and adversarial diffs, or it inherits the warm-signal bias and reports a recall number that is optimistic by construction.
- **The mechanism:** When building the golden-set corpus (Move 2), deliberately include adversarial/known-defective diffs (injected bugs, edge-case money inputs, malformed boundary data) rather than only curated real history. Treat "warm-audience bias" as a ranking input that says: assume internal recall numbers are an *upper bound*, size the human/CI net accordingly.
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence — Pass 3 R-4: [CANONICAL §8] "the harness has never been installed anywhere but event-vendor"; single real vendor (Monica). No external-user or adversarial-input validation channel exists.
- **Conservative disposition:** §C deferred / reframed-down. Pass 3 explicitly demoted it from "a new gate" to "a ranking input, not a new gate" — i.e. it folded R-4 into R-2 as a seeding caveat rather than a standalone move.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: UPHELD-CUT as a standalone gate; ELEVATE as a calibration-seeding requirement.** Pass 3's demotion is actually correct here, and this is a place to be honest rather than reflexively elevate. Building a *separate* "paying-stranger validation channel" as its own mechanism is premature — there is no paying stranger yet (single vendor, [CANONICAL §8]), so a standalone external-signal gate would be a speculative abstraction with no traffic to feed it (named failure mode: an empty channel that reports green because nothing flows through it — a forgeable gate of exactly the kind the essay warns against). BUT the underlying requirement — **adversarial seeding of the golden set** — must be elevated from "caveat" to a *hard construction rule* for Move 2, because a friendly-only corpus produces a recall number that is optimistic by construction and therefore a calibration that is itself authority-laundered. Cut the standalone channel; bind the adversarial-seeding requirement to Move 2 as non-optional.

## Autonomy angle

This source is, under the surface, **one of the most autonomy-relevant in the batch** — even though it never says "bug→PR" or "Slack summon." Its entire structural thesis (Pass 2's *authority laundering*) is a direct theory of *what breaks when you remove the human from the loop*. The essay's framing question — "if the human role compresses to oversight and verification, is the system actually best at verifying?" — is precisely the question the new charter's autonomy goal forces. Concretely, it teaches the **safety preconditions for self-improving loops and unattended cloud scheduling**: (1) the terminal stop authority must be CI-green-on-SHA, not model-agreement, or the autonomous loop converges on its own blind spots (Move 1); (2) the reviewer's miss rate must be *measured* before it is trusted as the backstop, because in autonomy there is no human backstop behind it (Move 2); (3) the loop will increasingly author its own oracle, so a human-authored, model-immutable spec (PBT invariants) is the only check the self-improving loop cannot weaken (Move 3). It does not teach the *trigger* mechanics (bug→PR, Slack/Linear summon, scheduler internals) — those live in sibling sources (`goal-loop-primitive`, `loop-engineering`). What it uniquely supplies is the **convergence/verification safety contract those triggers must satisfy** before they are allowed to merge unattended. Self-improving loops specifically: it is the cited warning that a loop graded by its own model family optimizes toward preference not truth (self-preference ~10%, arXiv:2410.21819) — the reason a self-improvement loop needs an external oracle in its stop condition.

## The single biggest move (one paragraph)

**Wire the unforgeable terminal stop authority (Move 1): make the autonomous loop's "done" require MUST-FIX=0 AND CI-required-checks-green on the sentinel'd SHA, enforced in CI/branch-protection where the loop cannot forge it.** This is the move the vision synthesis should pick up first because it is the literal hinge between the old charter and the new one. As long as a human frames, reviews, and merges every change, a self-certified convergence gate is a minor quality smell. The moment the charter makes autonomy first-class — bug→reviewed-PR, summoned from Slack/Linear, self-improving loops, cloud `/schedule` pushing while the laptop is closed — that self-certified gate becomes the *only* thing standing between "the model agreed with itself" and "shipped to `main`," and it is currently forgeable: the `.cr-ok` sentinel is gitignored and never reaches CI [CANONICAL §3f]. It is world-class because it operationalizes the essay's deepest insight — move the *terminal* authority outside the loop so it cannot be laundered — using machinery that already physically exists (the `.cr-ok` chain and a real CI oracle); only the wiring is missing. It is also the natural ordering anchor: R-1 makes the stop gate honest, R-2 measures how much the soft reviewer still leaks beneath it, and R-3 (PBT) supplies the first human-immutable spec the loop genuinely cannot redefine — together turning the harness's verification stack from "an elegant hope" into a control before any unattended autonomy is switched on.
