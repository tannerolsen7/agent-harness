# Ambition Re-mine — Loop Engineering: "You Built the Harness, Not the Loop"

## What this source is (plain English, 2-3 sentences)

A curation built on Addy Osmani's "Loop Engineering" essays plus the Claude Code `/goal` and scheduled-tasks docs. Its single load-bearing claim: a *harness* is a pipeline you invoke by hand (even a sophisticated multi-agent one); a *loop* is a system that finds its own work on a schedule and prompts the agents **for** you — and the **only** thing that converts the former into the latter is the heartbeat (a scheduled, self-triggering discovery step backed by on-disk memory). The page also confesses that its own first draft wrongly told the reader they "already had a loop," which it uses to argue that a static file-inventory audit *structurally cannot detect a missing heartbeat*.

## The full-ambition moves this source teaches

### Move: The heartbeat — a scheduled, self-triggering discovery step is the one wire that makes a harness a loop

- **Plain-English what & why:** Every mechanism in our harness fires only when a human starts a session and types something. There is no step that surfaces work *we didn't already know about*. A world-class loop runs on a clock with no human in the firing position: on a cadence it scans for regressions, drift, recurring findings, and open bugs, and prompts the agents to act. The failure it prevents is the silent death of every "weekly" or "scheduled" ritual — in a system with no scheduler, a "weekly ritual" runs twice and then never again. The work that depends on *noticing* (drift, recurring failure classes, accumulating tech debt) simply never gets noticed, because noticing was nobody's scheduled job.
- **The mechanism:** Wire a durable cloud scheduler (the `/schedule` skill + `CronCreate`, both confirmed present in this environment) to fire a recurring discovery agent. That agent runs the scans our rituals describe, reads the runtime-error surface, greps the review log for finding-classes recurring 3+ times, and deposits candidates somewhere a human (or, under the new charter, a gated action agent) consumes them. The clock is the deliverable — not more orchestration, not more skills.
- **Citation:** Gap 1, confirmed on-disk absence: "there is **no cron, no scheduled-tasks config, no `/loop` or `/goal` wiring** in `.claude/settings.json` or anywhere in `.claude/`" (pass3 §b). Corroborated by map §3e: rituals (`.claude/rituals.md`) "are triggered **only at session start**." The scheduling substrate is confirmed present: pass3 §d notes "the deferred-tool surface in *this very environment* lists `CronCreate` / `CronList` and a `schedule`/`loop` skill."
- **Conservative disposition:** §C DEFERRED, gated behind three conditions. Pass3 §d's recommendation: the `triage-inbox.md` node "maps to the map's candidate-build register as a §5-style 'build it' item, but **gated behind**: (i) naming a durable scheduler [closes C1], (ii) building `review-log.md` / the `/p/[token]` table [closes Gap 3], (iii) wiring the morning-review consumer [closes Gap 4]. Until those, it would be a fourth orphaned ritual." The whole proposal hinged on the unresolved durability worry: pass3 §d's "one narrow exception" was a "15-minute capability spike (`CronCreate` + observe)" to check whether the scheduler "fires when the dev's machine is asleep/closed."
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE — to the top of the in-scope-now list.** The single objection that gated this whole move — *does the scheduler fire when the laptop is closed?* — is now a **RESOLVED FACT**: cloud `/schedule` runs on Anthropic infra, clones the repo, runs committed skills, calls connectors, and explicitly "keeps working when laptop closed." The pass3 caveat ("a real heartbeat vs a second orphaned ritual") is answered: it is a real heartbeat. So the deferral collapses. Two of the three gating conditions also weaken under the charter: gate (i) "name a durable scheduler" is *done* (cloud `/schedule`); gate (iii) "wire the morning-review consumer" is dissolved by autonomy — under the new charter the loop doesn't deposit into a file a human might read on an unscheduled morning, it triggers a **gated action agent** (bug → reviewed PR) directly. Only gate (ii) — build the discovery inputs — remains real, and that is a build task, not a reason to defer the heartbeat. How much bigger: this goes from "deferred candidate, blocked on three gates" to **the foundational primitive of the entire V2 autonomy story** — every autonomy mechanism in the new charter (self-improving loops, summon-from-Slack drained by polling, scheduled bug sweeps) is a heartbeat with a different payload. The old reasoning was conservative bias of exactly the kind the charter names: it treated "machine asleep / durability" as a hard blocker when cloud scheduling had already mooted it.

### Move: The temporal-gap audit doctrine — a "scheduled ritual" with no named, always-on clock is indistinguishable on disk from one that fires, yet does nothing

- **Plain-English what & why:** This is the source's deepest and most transferable idea. A file-inventory audit can confirm `permission-logger.sh` *exists*; it cannot confirm that anything *ever reads its output on a schedule*. The gap is purely temporal — "does it fire on its own?" — and temporal gaps don't show up in a file inventory, because a heartbeat is not a file, it is a *trigger relationship between a clock and a file*. The failure it prevents is the most insidious one in self-assessment: looking at a sophisticated harness, pattern-matching "sophisticated → done," and shipping a system where a third of the "complete" controls are inert. The page proves this is real by confessing its own first draft made exactly that error.
- **The mechanism:** Add a **"fires how / on what clock?"** column to every ritual and hook row in the CANONICAL map and in any future harness audit. A row with no entry in that column is, by definition, a hope — not a control. This is a doctrine change to how we *audit*, not a single artifact: it makes the absence of a heartbeat structurally visible the way the current inventory cannot.
- **Citation:** pass2 §2.3 / §2.6.A: "a file-inventory audit (which is exactly what our CANONICAL-HARNESS-AS-IS map is) **structurally cannot detect a missing heartbeat**." pass3 §d recommendation: "promote pass2 §2.6.A (temporal-gap rule) into the audit doctrine — *the CANONICAL map must add a 'fires how / on what clock?' column to every ritual/hook row*... That single doctrine change is the article's highest-value contribution to V2, above the `triage-inbox.md` node itself." Corroborates map §3e's static verdict: "the system is overwhelmingly advisory."
- **Conservative disposition:** Fully-applied (this is the one move pass3 enthusiastically endorsed) — pass3 §d calls it "the article's highest-value contribution to V2." But it was framed as a *documentation/audit-hygiene* improvement (add a column to a map), not as an enforcement mechanism.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE — from audit hygiene to an enforced harness invariant.** Pass3 correctly identified this as the highest-value idea, but under the conservative charter it could only land as "add a column to a markdown table." Under "clarity beats minimalism" and "autonomy is first-class," the temporal-gap rule should be **enforced, not documented**: a CI/lint check (or a `/cr` pass) that fails when a ritual is declared in `rituals.md` without a corresponding scheduled trigger (a `/schedule` routine or `CronCreate` entry). That is the Pillar-1 spirit applied to itself — "a control with no enforcing hook is a hope" should not itself be a hope. How much bigger: from a passive column in one audit document to a standing **invariant that no committed ritual may exist without a clock**, checked mechanically. This is not a phantom — the map confirms the gap is real and unenforced (§3e "overwhelmingly advisory"); the upgrade is from describing the rule to wiring it.

### Move: Split autonomy at the discover/act seam — automate discovery, and (under the new charter) route action through a gated, disable-model-invocation action agent rather than the human

- **Plain-English what & why:** The source draws its caution from our own Svpino R1 experiment: overnight full autonomy *relocated the review bottleneck* (+98% PRs, +154% PR size, +9% bug density, **zero DORA gain**). The lesson is "don't generate more than you can review." So the source prescribes: automate the *discovery* half (cheap, safe — surfacing candidates), keep the *action* half gated. The failure it prevents is a fleet that floods you with unreviewable output and nets zero throughput gain.
- **The mechanism:** The discovery loop surfaces candidate work; action flows through a controlled gate with blast-radius caps. Under the new charter the gate is not a human's unscheduled morning review — it is a **`disable-model-invocation: true` action skill** (open-PR / deploy / send-Slack), invisible to the discovering model's context, so the discovery agent *cannot* fire a side effect by accident. The seam is preserved; the consumer of the discovery output changes from "a human habit" to "a deterministic gated agent that produces a *reviewed* PR."
- **Citation:** pass1 Claim 9 (R1 metrics) and pass2 §2.6.B (the discover/act split). pass3 §a confirms our current posture already biases to gated action: "`/change`, `/queue`, `/compound` curation are human-initiated... so the article's prescription (gate action) is *already our posture*; we would only be adding the discovery half." The `disable-model-invocation` mechanism is a charter RESOLVED FACT ("removes a skill from the model's context entirely → makes side-effect skills safe").
- **Conservative disposition:** §C/§E mixed — partly already-built (gated action is "already our posture," pass3 §a), and the discovery half deferred as part of the gated `triage-inbox` node. Pass2 §2.2 also raised it as an *unreconciled tension* (C3): "a discovery loop *generates review candidates*... A triage inbox that surfaces 30 candidate items a morning is a new review burden wearing a discovery costume." Pass3 §c.2 upheld that worry: "It over-credits 'discovery' as the scarce resource... A discovery loop that floods a triage inbox adds to exactly that curation load."
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE the discover/act *seam*, but the C3 "review is the scarce resource" objection is itself partially UPHELD — and that tension is exactly what the autonomy design must solve, not wave away.** The seam is correct and world-class (it is literally the R1-respecting shape). What changes under the charter is the *consumer* of discovery output: the new charter's "bug → reviewed PR" means discovery feeds a gated action agent that produces a *reviewable artifact* (a PR with passing CI and a `/cr` pass), not 30 raw triage items dumped on a human. That converts the C3 failure mode ("a new review burden wearing a discovery costume") from a blocker into a *design constraint*: the loop must produce **review-cheap output** (a single scoped PR per finding, pre-validated), not a flood. So I ELEVATE the seam (in-scope-now, foundational to autonomy) while UPHOLDING the substance of C3 as a named failure mode the design must engineer against — a discovery loop with no output-shaping discipline genuinely does relocate the bottleneck, which is the one thing R1 proves is worthless.

### Move: Distinguish `/loop` (cadence re-run) from `/goal` (run-until-graded-stopping-condition) — and recognize neither is the loop, but both are loop *primitives* the harness lacks

- **Plain-English what & why:** Two Claude Code primitives that our harness does not wire. `/loop` re-runs a prompt or skill on a clock (the ritual layer, *if it had a scheduler*). `/goal` runs a single task until a verifiable stopping condition graded by a *separate* model — autonomous continuation on one task, not discovery. The source's point is that *neither alone is the loop* (the loop is the automation that fires them), but together they are the missing in-session autonomy primitives: `/goal` lets an agent grind a bounded task to a graded "done" without a human re-prompting each turn.
- **The mechanism:** Wire `/goal` so a long-running task (e.g. "make this flaky integration suite green," "drive this migration to all-green tests") runs until a separate grader model confirms the stopping condition, bottoming out on our CI/`.cr-ok` sentinel — instead of stopping after one turn and waiting for a human "continue." `/loop` becomes the cadence wrapper for in-session repeated checks.
- **Citation:** pass1 Claim 8 (the `/loop` vs `/goal` distinction; `/goal`'s grader "must bottom out on your CI sentinel"). Confirmed absence: Gap 1 — "no `/loop` or `/goal` wiring" anywhere in `.claude/` (pass3 §b). The grader-bottoms-out-on-sentinel hook to our `.cr-ok` chain is a real seam: map §3f notes "the Node 8.5(c) gap (CI never verifies `.cr-ok`)."
- **Conservative disposition:** §C DEFERRED / effectively summarized away. Pass3 treats `/loop` and `/goal` only as definitional context for the heartbeat ("Neither is the loop"); it never proposes wiring `/goal` as an autonomy primitive in its own right. The charter prompt's §C list explicitly names "the `/goal` loop primitive" among the biggest ideas that "died" in deferral.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: NEW + ELEVATE.** The conservative synthesis used `/goal` as a *foil* ("it's not the loop") and never recovered it as a capability we lack and want. Under "autonomy is first-class," a graded run-until-done primitive is core: it is what lets a summoned bug-fix agent grind to a verified green state without a human re-prompting every turn, and it gives the grader a *separate-model* check that complements `/cr`. The R1 caution applies here too — `/goal`'s grader must bottom out on a real sentinel (CI + `/cr`), not on the agent's own transcript-only self-grade (pass1 Claim 8 flags this: "transcript-only and must bottom out on your CI sentinel"). Recover it as a first-class candidate: wire `/goal` with a CI/`.cr-ok`-anchored grader. This is genuinely NEW relative to MASTER-FINDINGS, which only kept the heartbeat-vs-orchestration framing.

## Autonomy angle

This source is **the most directly autonomy-relevant article in the corpus** — autonomy is its entire subject. Everything it teaches maps to the new charter's autonomy pillars:

- **Self-improving loops / cloud scheduling:** The heartbeat (Move 1) *is* the scheduling primitive. The source asserted scheduling "exists in this environment" and pass3 confirmed it (`CronCreate`, `CronList`, `/schedule`, `/loop`). The charter resolves the one durability doubt the source couldn't (laptop-closed → cloud `/schedule` runs on Anthropic infra). This source supplies the *spine* on which "self-improving loops" and "cloud scheduling" are built.
- **Bug → reviewed PR:** Move 3 (discover/act seam) + the `disable-model-invocation` gated action agent is precisely the bug→PR pipeline. The discovery loop reads the runtime-error surface and finding-class recurrences; the gated action agent produces a *reviewed* PR. The source's R1 caution ("don't generate more than you can review") is the design constraint that keeps bug→PR from becoming the worthless +98%-PRs-zero-DORA outcome.
- **Slack/Linear summon:** Not addressed by name. The source's model is clock-driven discovery, not inbound-trigger-driven. But a summon front-door is mechanically the same heartbeat with a different sensor (poll Slack/Linear instead of poll the clock), so the heartbeat primitive generalizes to it.
- **`/goal` graded continuation (Move 4):** the in-session autonomy primitive that lets a summoned or scheduled agent run a bounded task to a *graded* done.

The source's standing rule ("a control described as a scheduled ritual with no scheduler is a hope") is the doctrinal backbone for evaluating every autonomy claim in V2: an autonomy mechanism with no clock or trigger wired to it does not exist.

## The single biggest move (one paragraph)

**Wire the cloud heartbeat (Move 1) — the scheduled, self-triggering discovery step — and make it the foundation of the autonomy story, not a deferred node.** The conservative synthesis deferred this behind three gates, the binding one being a durability doubt ("does the scheduler fire when the laptop is closed?"). That doubt is now a resolved fact: cloud `/schedule` runs on Anthropic infrastructure, clones the repo, runs committed skills, and keeps working with the laptop shut. So the single wire the source says separates a harness from a loop — and which we confirmably lack (Gap 1: no cron, no `/loop`/`/goal`, rituals fire only at session start) — is buildable *today* with a substrate confirmed present in this very environment. It is world-class because it is the minimal, R1-respecting primitive on which every other autonomy mechanism in the new charter (bug→PR, summon-drain, self-improving loops) is just a heartbeat with a different sensor and a different gated payload. Paired with the temporal-gap audit doctrine (Move 2, elevated to an *enforced* invariant so no ritual may exist without a clock), it ensures the harness can never again silently regress into a pile of documented-but-inert "scheduled" rituals — which is exactly the failure the map's "overwhelmingly advisory" verdict already records.
