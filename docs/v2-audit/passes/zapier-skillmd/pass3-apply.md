## (b) REAL gaps it exposes — each citing ground-truth

1. **No SKILL.md frontmatter / no skill router — the article's headline gap is real for us.** Ground-truth §1 states plainly: when the agent sees `/cr`, `/queue`, `/dev`, `/tdd`, `Notion:*` in one list, "**it cannot tell which layer owns each, nor which will travel to another project.**" That is exactly the discoverability problem frontmatter solves. Our skills are Markdown-body only with no machine-readable `triggers` / `required-tools` / `owning-layer` contract. **Gap confirmed via §1 (runtime skill list mixes all layers, no ownership signal) and §3b (26 disk skills, no registry).** BUT — applying pass2 §2.2 — frontmatter is inert without a consumer; ground-truth §1 confirms **no router exists**. So the real gap is two-part: (i) add a frontmatter contract, (ii) build/decide the thing that reads it. Proposing (i) alone would be the "documentation, not infrastructure" trap pass2 named.

2. **`block-dangerous-bash.sh` absent — the deterministic safety floor the article's governance thesis demands.** Ground-truth §3e and §5 record this as **canon's 3rd structural guard (deploys, `rm -rf`, writes to `.git`/`.husky`/`.claude`), ABSENT on disk** — "Disk has no safety-floor bash guard." Pass2 §2.1 tier-1 (deterministic) + §2.3 (un-watched sessions) make this the most defensible gap the article surfaces: for **unattended agents** (ground-truth UNATTENDED worktree mode), a destructive-op rule that lives only in CLAUDE.md prose is **mis-tiered** by pass2's test — advisory enforcement for a receiver that isn't watched. The article's "you can't enforce policy in a session that's not being watched" lands directly on this absence.

3. **`enforce-scope.sh` absent — structural backstop for the article's "action scoping."** Ground-truth §3e/§5: **canon-structural, absent on disk** (blocks staging files outside TASK-TEMPLATE `## ALLOWED FILES`). Zapier's MCP "action scoping" is the infra analogue of file-scope enforcement. Today our scope rule is advisory CLAUDE.md prose; the deterministic version is unbuilt. Cited absence: §5 row `enforce-scope.sh`.

4. **The enforcement layer is overwhelmingly advisory — the article's central distinction is our central unmet need.** Ground-truth §3e "Net enforcement picture": "**Both agree the system is overwhelmingly advisory — neither has a deterministic backstop for the bulk of skill bodies, CLAUDE.md rules, or the autoMode lists.**" This is pass2 §2.1's typology validated by our own map: most of our rules sit in tier-3 (advisory) with no tier-1 backstop. The article gives us the vocabulary (policy vs infrastructure) to prioritize which advisory rules most need promotion — and pass2 §2.7 gives the test (match tier to receiver). This is a framing gap the article fills, anchored to a real §3e finding.

5. **"Questions not directives" — narrow, conditional, real only for human-facing output.** Applying pass2 §2.2's contradiction (Socratic for humans, deterministic for agents): `/cr`'s MUST-FIX items are auto-fixed by Opus (§3c) — that path should stay *imperative*, not Socratic (a question to an auto-fixer is mis-tiered). The only place the article's rule applies is the **human-facing NEEDS-HUMAN / SUGGESTION output**. So this is a *small, scoped* gap at most, not the blanket "add to /cr" the article proposes — and it must target `/cr` (the live skill), not `/cr-feature`.
