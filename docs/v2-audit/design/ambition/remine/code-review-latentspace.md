# Ambition Re-mine -- Code Review in the Agent Era (Latent Space + 15-Company Survey)

## What this source is (plain English, 2-3 sentences)

A 2026 Latent Space analysis (Ankit Jain / Aviator) plus a survey of 15+ companies (GitHub, Vercel, Cognition/Devin, Cursor, Greptile, Graphite, Ona, Bitloops, Google Gemini CLI, Microsoft, et al.) about what code review becomes when agents write most of the code. Its real thesis — buried under the "how to review better" framing — is that review-as-a-late-stage-human-gate should mostly cease to exist: verification moves *upstream* (spec gate, deterministic invariants), *sideways* (an adversarial peer with independent framing), and *out of the human path* (deterministic risk-based auto-approval), so the human gate shrinks to a confirmatory glance. The strongest evidence in it is mechanism-level and quantified — Bitloops (87-100% violation reduction over 8 weeks via context accumulation), Google Gemini CLI #26397 (43%→91% merge readiness via 3-4 rounds of cross-model adversarial iteration), and Microsoft's 400/800-line review-effectiveness cliff.

## The full-ambition moves this source teaches

### Move: REJECT as a first-class outcome — the harness can refuse the approach, not just polish it

- **Plain-English what & why:** Today `/cr` has three exits: MUST FIX (auto-fixed), NEEDS HUMAN, SUGGESTION. All three assume the PR is *worth finishing* and just needs work. There is no exit that says "this is the wrong approach — close it and re-spec." The article calls this "not a minor gap." The failure it prevents is the most insidious agentic-pipeline failure mode: an overnight `/queue` run produces five beautifully-polished, all-tests-green PRs that solve the *wrong problem*, touch files outside scope, or balloon past the reviewable-diff cliff — and because the system has no concept of approach-level failure, it ships them up the chain to a tired human at 9 AM instead of killing and re-queuing them automatically. "Functionally correct, wrong problem" is invisible to a reviewer reading the diff cold; it is only visible when the diff is checked *against the spec*.
- **The mechanism:** Add a `REJECT` classification to `/cr` with deterministic triggers: (1) scope explosion — files touched outside the spec's declared scope; (2) diff exceeds a hard ceiling (article's default 800 lines, treat as tunable); (3) CI failing with no auto-fix path (the strongest single predictor of non-merge); (4) auth/schema/payment change with zero or negative test delta; (5) the diff demonstrably solves a different problem than the spec states. A REJECT closes the PR automatically and re-queues the task with a reason note — it does *not* escalate to a human. In autonomous mode this is the safety valve that keeps a self-driving pipeline from drowning the human in confidently-wrong work.
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence. Ground-truth §3c: disk `/cr` has **"No REJECT tier, no UNATTENDED branching."** CLAUDE.md pipeline resolution lists only MUST FIX / NEEDS HUMAN / SUGGESTION.
- **Conservative disposition:** pass3 (b)(1) confirmed this as a real gap but framed it narrowly: "build the tier, treat the thresholds as defaults to tune." MASTER-FINDINGS folded it into the cautious "wiring-tweak" set. The conservative read treated REJECT as a *fourth label on an existing review skill* — a classification addition.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE.** The conservative read undersized this by treating it as a label. Under "autonomy is first-class," REJECT is not a label — it is the *control surface that makes an autonomous loop safe to run unattended*. A bug→PR pipeline summoned from Slack/Linear and run on cloud `/schedule` with the laptop closed has exactly one catastrophic failure mode: it ships polished wrong work and the human can't tell at a glance. REJECT-and-re-queue is the autonomous analogue of a circuit breaker. It must be: (a) deterministic (so `disable-model-invocation` side-effect skills can act on it without model judgment), (b) wired into `/queue` so re-queue is automatic, (c) bounded by a re-queue attempt cap (REJECT→re-spec→REJECT twice = escalate to human, don't infinite-loop). That is three mechanisms, not one label. The "tune the thresholds later" framing is correct on the *numbers* (pass2 §C: the article admits no industry consensus then names a number anyway) but conservative on the *architecture*: the loop topology — close, annotate, re-queue, cap, escalate-on-repeat — should be designed in now, not deferred.

### Move: True adversarial independence — fresh-context reviewer with project canon but isolated solution context

- **Plain-English what & why:** The single strongest *quantified mechanism* in the entire source is Google Gemini CLI #26397: cross-model adversarial iteration (model A writes, model B hunts bugs, A fixes, B retests, 3-4 rounds) lifted merge-readiness from 43% to 91% on a test PR set *before any human looked*. The principle is "the auditor doesn't prepare the books" — a reviewer that shares the coder's context inherits the coder's blind spots and rationalizations. Our `/cr` adversarial pass runs *inside the same `/cr` invocation over the same branch diff*, sharing the coding session's context window. It is structurally incapable of the independence that produced the 43%→91% gain. But — and the article misses this — pure "no shared context" would also blind the reviewer to our Rejected Patterns and project canon, which is exactly what makes `/cr` good (pass1 §7). The right design the article never states: **shared project canon, isolated solution context.**
- **The mechanism:** Provision the adversarial pass as a *separate sub-agent* with a clean prompt and clean solution context (it does NOT see the coding session's reasoning, the author's justifications, or the chain of edits) but WITH read access to project canon (CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, Rejected Patterns, PITFALLS, ADRs). Its task framing is inverted: "find what breaks this," not "review this." Run it as the documented 3-4 round loop (hunt→fix→retest) before the human review window, not a single pass. This is cheap — a fresh sub-agent with a clean prompt is a context-isolation change, not a different-model architecture change (pass2 §D: the article conflates context-isolation / fresh-prompt / different-model as if they were one thing; the cheapest one is sufficient).
- **Citation:** Confirmed gap. Ground-truth §3c: the adversarial pass runs "as part of the same `/cr` invocation over the full branch diff" — no separate-context provisioning is recorded; the 4 lenses run inside `/cr`. The 4 lens agents do exist on disk [§3d].
- **Conservative disposition:** pass3 (b)(2) named this a real gap and the correct fix ("shared project canon, isolated solution context"), but MASTER-FINDINGS collapsed it into the wiring-tweak set and pass3 (d) demoted it to "one bounded fact-check" — verify what independence produced the Gemini gain before committing. It treated the iteration *count* (3-4 rounds) as optional polish.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE.** This is the highest-leverage *evidenced* move in the source and the conservative synthesis buried it as a fact-check. 43%→91% is the largest pre-human quality jump cited anywhere, and it is the mechanism most directly aligned with the autonomy north star: an autonomous bug→PR loop with no human in the inner cycle *needs* an independent verifier doing multiple rounds, because there is no human to catch the rationalization. Bigger than the conservative read in three ways: (1) it is not a single fresh-prompt pass — it is the *iterative loop* (hunt→fix→retest→repeat to convergence or cap), which the conservative read dropped; (2) the isolated-solution-context-with-shared-canon split is a genuine architectural pattern, not a prompt tweak; (3) it should be the *default inner loop of autonomous runs*, gating PR-open. The "bounded fact-check first" caution is fine to keep, but it should not block designing the loop — the fact-check (different model vs fresh prompt) only changes the *cost knob*, not whether we build it.

### Move: Deterministic risk-based auto-approval — take most PRs out of the human path entirely (Ona's L4)

- **Plain-English what & why:** This is the move the article *contains* but underweights, and the one pass2 §F identified as the real answer the article dodges. Ona ships a deterministic risk-classification layer that auto-approves low-risk PRs with no human review at all, and reports a **74% lead-time reduction**. The classification is deterministic — based on paths touched, diff size, and whether auth/schema/payment is touched — not an LLM opinion. The failure the article's *default* framing commits, and that Ona avoids: every other recommendation assumes "cap PRs to what a human can review in a 2-hour window" (the "5 PRs <400 lines" math). That is a human-throughput model bolted onto a machine-throughput pipeline. When agents generate faster than any human can review, capping output to human review capacity is solving the wrong problem. The right answer is to make *most PRs not require human review at all*, and reserve scarce human attention for the high-blast-radius minority.
- **The mechanism:** A deterministic risk-classifier (a hook / script, not a skill — so it is safe under `disable-model-invocation` and runs with no model judgment) that reads the diff and emits a risk tier from: paths touched (does it hit auth / middleware / RLS / schema / payment / `next.config.ts`?), diff size, test delta, and whether files are inside declared scope. LOW-risk PRs (small, in-scope, no blast-radius paths, positive test delta, CI green, `/cr` clean) auto-merge or auto-mark-approved with no human gate. MEDIUM escalates to the existing `/cr` flow. HIGH (auth/schema/payment) forces `/cr-security` + mandatory human sign-off — this is L4 "blast-radius auto-escalation" made *enforced* rather than advisory.
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence. Ground-truth has no risk-classification layer and no auto-approve path anywhere in canon or disk. The blast-radius escalation we *do* have is advisory only — ground-truth §3e Net: the system is "overwhelmingly advisory"; CLAUDE.md merely *instructs* running `/cr-security` "alongside" auth/middleware/RLS commits, with no deterministic enforcement. The deterministic guards that would underpin a classifier (`block-dangerous-bash.sh`, `enforce-scope.sh`, `branch-registry-guard.sh`) are **canon-structural, ABSENT on disk** [§3e, §5].
- **Conservative disposition:** Scaled-down / underweighted. pass2 §F correctly identified Ona/L4 as "the better answer the article underweights," and pass3 (c)(7) repeated it as a *weakness in the article's reasoning* — but neither pass3 (b) gaps nor MASTER-FINDINGS turned it into a build candidate. It was treated as "interesting critique of the article," not "thing we should build." The closest the conservative synthesis came was noting L4 is "partial, manually-triggered" (pass3 (a)(3)).
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE (toward NEW — it was never made a candidate).** This is arguably the most charter-aligned move in the source and the conservative synthesis left it on the floor as commentary. "Remove most PRs from the human path via deterministic auto-approval" IS the autonomy thesis applied to the merge gate. For a world-class engineer running parallel agent fleets across 5+ repos, the human-review-capacity model is *the* bottleneck the whole vision is trying to dissolve — capping output to "5 PRs a human can read" is precisely the conservative solo-dev framing the new charter retires. The 74% lead-time number is from an independent operator, not a vendor self-report, so it survives the pass2 §H epistemic discount. The reason this wasn't elevated before is exactly the suppressed bias: the conservative synthesis kept the human as the mandatory final gate. Under "autonomy designed in, never deferred," the risk-classifier-with-auto-approve is in-scope-now. Real caveat (keep the rigor): for a $30k-client proposal tool the *threshold* for what counts as LOW-risk should be conservative, and auto-merge must require CI-green + `/cr`-clean + in-scope + positive-test-delta simultaneously — but that tunes the gate, it does not reject the mechanism.

### Move: The compounding read-path — review findings must feed back into task-start context, with a freshness model

- **Plain-English what & why:** Bitloops' result is the source's other strong quantified mechanism: over 8 weeks, recording every caught violation and feeding it back as generation context drove security violations down 87%, architectural 92%, compliance 100% — and the finding was explicit that the improvement came from *context accumulation*, not model updates. This is the engine of the "Stage 1 → Stage 4 / agent PRs approved on first review" path. The failure it prevents: an agent that makes the same class of mistake every run because nothing it gets corrected on is ever read back at the start of the next run. Our harness has the *write* side (RECURRING-FINDINGS.md is auto-counted by `/cr` Step 3b) but ground-truth is blunt that it is **"never read by implementers"** [§4]. We accumulate findings into a store no one reads. That is a write-only loop — the compounding never closes.
- **The mechanism:** Close the read-path: review findings (the recurring ones, the project-specific corrections) must be loaded into the *implementer's* task-start context, not just counted in a pipeline-only file. Critically — and this is what the article omits and our own canon supplies — pair it with an **eviction/freshness model**: a monotonically-growing learned store is a context-budget liability (pass2 §E), the exact "scaffold the model no longer needs" the Model Capacity Audit pre-authorizes removing [§9: "ghost rules if unobserved 90 days; collapse + quarterly audit"]. The "violation recurrence rate" metric the article names is actually the *eviction signal*: a pattern that stops recurring can be retired. So: read recurring findings at task start, decay them by recurrence (90-day unobserved → collapse), and do NOT build a new `learned-patterns.md` file (that is a §6 phantom and would duplicate `docs/solutions/` + `RECURRING-FINDINGS.md`).
- **Citation:** Confirmed read-path gap. Ground-truth §4: RECURRING-FINDINGS.md is "pipeline-only, auto-counted by `/cr` Step 3b, **never read by implementers**." The freshness rule already exists in canon [§9]. The `learned-patterns.md` the article recommends is a §6 phantom — do not build it.
- **Conservative disposition:** pass3 (b)(6) handled this well — correctly flagged the `learned-patterns.md` trap and reframed it as a read-path gap. But MASTER-FINDINGS folded it into the wiring-tweak set, and the conservative framing treated it as a documentation/plumbing fix ("wire the read path").
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: ELEVATE (modestly).** The conservative diagnosis is correct and should be kept — this is the rare case where pass3 nailed it. The elevation is in *priority and scope*, not direction: under "self-improving loops are first-class," the closed compounding loop (write findings → read at task start → decay by recurrence → measure first-pass approval to confirm it's working) IS the self-improvement primitive. Bitloops' 87-100% is the proof it works. The conservative synthesis treated it as plumbing; the charter makes it a load-bearing autonomy mechanism — the thing that turns "agent that needs 2-4 review rounds" into "agent approved on first review." It is bigger than a read-path wire because it is incomplete without the measurement layer (next move) — you cannot compound what you don't measure.

### Move: An effectiveness-measurement layer (first-pass approval rate, cycle count, per-task-type) — NEW

- **Plain-English what & why:** Every source in the survey that *improved* (Bitloops, Gemini CLI, Elementor, Ona) measured something and watched it move. The article's framework names the specific metrics: **first-pass approval rate** (the primary quality signal), review-cycle-count per PR, post-merge defect rate attributed to *agent* PRs tracked separately from human PRs, violation-recurrence rate, and PR-size trend (a leading indicator of future bottleneck). The failure this prevents: "review skills deployed then evaluated anecdotally." Our entire memory/metrics model [ground-truth §4] is about *knowledge stores* — there is no row, in canon or disk, for first-pass-approval-rate or cycle-count or post-merge-defect tracking. We have no idea whether `/cr` is getting better or worse over time, whether the compounding loop is actually compounding, or which task types the agent one-shots vs flails on. This is the gap that *blocks* every other improvement loop: you cannot run a self-improving harness on vibes.
- **The mechanism:** A lightweight metrics layer (a JSON ledger in the repo, or a Notion/GitHub-canon table under the new GitHub-as-source-of-truth model) that records, per agent PR: task type, first-pass approval (did `/cr` pass clean on attempt 1?), number of review cycles, REJECT-or-not, post-merge defect attribution, recurrence of each finding. Surfaced as a periodic report (the cloud `/schedule` can run this weekly with the laptop closed). The metrics close the loops: first-pass-approval-rate tells you if compounding works; recurrence-rate is the eviction signal for the learned store; PR-size-trend is the leading indicator for when the diff-gate thresholds need tuning; per-task-type tells you which work is safe to auto-approve.
- **Citation:** Confirmed absence. Ground-truth §4: the memory/metrics model is "about *knowledge* stores, not *effectiveness* metrics — there is no row, in canon or disk, for first-pass-approval-rate, review-cycle-count, or post-merge-defect tracking."
- **Conservative disposition:** pass3 (b)(5) confirmed the absence and even named it "the gap that blocks the compounding" — but MASTER-FINDINGS did NOT carry it into the 6 cautious moves. It was summarized away. The conservative synthesis acknowledged the gap and then dropped it.
- **RE-JUDGMENT under the new charter: NEW (recover as first-class).** This was confirmed-real in pass3 and then dropped from the final 6 — the textbook case of the conservative synthesis suppressing a big move. Under the new charter it is not optional: a self-improving loop, by definition, requires a feedback signal, and first-pass-approval-rate IS that signal. "Outcome/impact tracking" is on the deferred list the charter explicitly says should move in-scope-now. For a fleet across 5+ repos, the measurement layer is also the only way to know *which repo's harness is regressing* — it is the dashboard for the whole fleet. The conservative bias here was treating measurement as a nice-to-have reporting feature rather than as the control input every other loop depends on. Build it; it is the keystone that makes the other four moves verifiable instead of aspirational.

## Autonomy angle

This source is **densely** about autonomy — more than its "code review" title suggests. Concretely:

- **Bug→PR pipeline:** Cursor's background agents (clone repo in isolated Ubuntu VMs, run against a spec, open a PR, up to 8 parallel) are described as the existing topology; the overnight `/queue` workflow (write spec by EOD → agent runs unattended → wake to branch + verification report) is the same loop. The article's structural `/queue` changes — diff-size hard gate, CI-pass-before-PR-open, file-scope enforcement, machine-generated PR summary, REJECT-and-re-queue — are precisely the *guardrails an autonomous bug→PR loop needs to be safe*.
- **Self-improving loops:** Bitloops' context-accumulation result (87-100% reduction over 8 weeks) and Gemini CLI's iterative adversarial loop (43%→91%) are both direct evidence for self-improving loops; the compounding read-path + effectiveness-metrics moves above are how we'd build them.
- **Cloud scheduling:** The "convert ~15h of idle overnight compute into review-ready PRs" argument maps exactly onto cloud `/schedule` running with the laptop closed. The metrics report and the nightly `/queue` cap are natural scheduled-agent jobs.
- **Slack/Linear summon:** Not directly addressed — the source assumes the spec/queue is the entry point, not a chat summon. (No claim here.)

The deterministic risk-classifier (Ona/L4) is the single most autonomy-critical mechanism: it is what lets an autonomous pipeline *merge* without a human, which is the difference between "agent that opens PRs a human must still review" and "agent that ships."

## The single biggest move (one paragraph)

**The deterministic risk-based auto-approval layer (Ona's L4): a non-LLM classifier that takes most PRs out of the human path entirely and reserves human attention for the blast-radius minority.** Pick this up first because it is the move that most directly realizes the new charter's north star — autonomy as first-class — and the conservative synthesis left it on the floor as mere commentary on the article's reasoning rather than a build candidate. Every other recommendation in the source (and every move in the conservative synthesis) quietly assumes the human is the mandatory final gate and then tries to ration the human's time ("5 PRs <400 lines in a 2h window"). That is the solo-dev bottleneck framing the charter explicitly retires. Ona's independent (non-vendor) 74% lead-time reduction proves the alternative works: classify risk deterministically by paths-touched + diff-size + test-delta + scope, auto-approve LOW, route MEDIUM to `/cr`, force `/cr-security` + human sign-off on HIGH (auth/schema/payment). It is world-class because it is deterministic (safe under `disable-model-invocation`, no model judgment in the merge decision), it makes blast-radius escalation *enforced* rather than advisory (closing ground-truth §3e's "overwhelmingly advisory" gap), and it is the load-bearing mechanism for a fleet of agents shipping across 5+ repos — without it, the human is the throughput ceiling no amount of parallel agents can lift.
