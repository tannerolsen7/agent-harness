## (c) Weaknesses in the article's OWN reasoning

- **It oversells CI-green as a truth oracle.** Pass2 §"load-bearing assumption" #2: CI-green certifies only the checks that exist; "MUST-FIX=0 AND CI-green" is a *coverage-bounded* oracle, not the clean external authority the prose implies. The article's strongest prescriptive sentence ("the stop authority must be the CI oracle") is true only modulo coverage — and our coverage is unmeasured (R-2). The cure partially depends on the thing it says is broken.
- **It ignores that the oracle is increasingly model-authored.** Pass2 §"load-bearing assumption" #1: if >80% of merged code is model-written (the article's own headline), then CI tests and properties are too. The article's cure (PBT, CI gating) re-internalizes the loop one level down and it never notices. The defensible core — *a human specifies the invariant* — is the real scarce resource, and the article mislabels the scarce capability as "verification" when pass2 shows it is "human-authored, model-immutable specification."
- **It conflates "calibrated" with "promotable to authority."** Pass2 §"contradiction": Finding 1 demotes the soft model gate below the hard oracle, then Finding 2 invests heavily in calibrating that same soft gate, without stating that a calibrated `/cr` is a *triage* layer (reduces human load), never a *terminal* authority. Build R-2 as triage calibration, not as a path to letting `/cr` self-certify.
- **Its borrowed-statistic transfer is loose.** The 16.6%-adopted / >50%-wrong figure [arXiv:2603.15911] measures *suggestion adoption in a specific agentic-review study*, not our `/cr`'s defect recall. The article itself flags the "18-20% ceiling" as an approximate paraphrase (pass1 §"sources"). Honest reading: these numbers establish *that* automated review under-performs and *that* recall must be measured locally — they do **not** give us our number. R-2 (measure locally) is the only valid inheritance; do not import 16.6% / "1-in-5" as if it were our recall.
