# Pass 3 — Apply: against the ground-truth harness map

**Building on pass2:** pass-2 established that this article's durable content is *not* its tool catalog (which rots — Pipedream's owner already changed, Airflow already EOL'd) but four tool-agnostic decision disciplines it never names as a unit: (1) refuse cross-category comparison, (2) price the fine print — labor / operations / license / owner-drift / sponsored-metrics — as a first-class cost, (3) name a failure mode the feature prevents or treat it as overhead, (4) distinguish reversible from irreversible commitments. Pass-2 closed by observing the article is doing to a *tool market* what the V2 audit is doing to a *harness*. This pass tests that — honestly, including where the analogy fails — against `CANONICAL-HARNESS-AS-IS.md`.

The honest headline up front: **this is a buyer's guide for SaaS automation platforms. Zero of its tools are in our stack and none belong in it.** Our "automation" is a Claude Code coding harness — `.claude/` skills, hooks, agents, git hooks, CI (canon §1, disk §2). n8n/Make/Pipedream/Temporal solve trigger→action and durable-execution problems we do not have. So the application is *method-level only*, and I will not manufacture tool-level "gaps." Most of this article's apparent relevance is a mirage; the small real signal is isolated in (b).

## (a) What we ALREADY do — the article's disciplines are already our audit's spine

The four pass-2 disciplines are not new to us; the ground-truth map already runs on them. Citing rows:

1. **"Name a failure mode the feature prevents, or it's overhead"** is *verbatim* our canon's golden rule: *"If you can't name a failure mode that the constraint prevents, the constraint is overhead."* [canon §9, reproduced from Page 13]. The article's "when NOT to use it" sections are the same move applied to tools that §9 applies to harness scaffolds. **Already do — convergent, not additive.**

2. **"Distinguish reversible from irreversible commitments"** is already load-bearing for us twice over: the Model Capacity Audit's keep/replace split turns on it (safety rules kept *because* their failure is irreversible — PocketOS) [canon §9], and our own estimation doctrine is explicitly "lead with architectural risk and reversibility, not weeks" [user memory: estimation harness-first]. The article's acquisition-risk / lock-in / license-scope analysis is the same one-way-door reasoning. **Already do.**

3. **"Refuse cross-category comparison"** maps to our map's central discipline: *no proposal survives without a citation to a row* — i.e. an item must be placed in the correct register (canon-only §5, disk-only §6, duplication §4, proxy §9) before it can be argued about [map §0, "How later phases cite this map"]. We already refuse to compare a canon ideal against a disk mechanism as if they were the same kind of thing. **Already do.**

4. **"Price the fine print — labor as first-class cost"** maps to our harness-first cadence: estimates are not in human-developer weeks because the user runs parallel agents + /queue batches [user memory: estimation harness-first; feedback_estimation]. We already refuse the naive headline number (dev-weeks) the way the article refuses Make's "$10.59/month." **Already do.**

Conclusion for (a): on *method*, the article and our audit are near-isomorphic. It is independent corroboration that our audit's disciplines are the right ones — useful as confidence, not as new instruction.

## (b) REAL gaps — each tied to a ground-truth row or confirmed absence

I find **three** defensible method-level gaps and one genuine tooling-adjacent signal. Each cites a row. I am deliberately conservative; weak analogies are quarantined in (c).

1. **"Price the fine print" is applied to external vendors but not to our own upstream dependency — the Matt-Pocock skill coupling is an un-priced owner-drift risk.** The article's Pipedream/Workday lesson (Mistake 4): a tool you build on can have its roadmap captured by an owner whose incentives diverge from yours. Our map records exactly this shape and does *not* price it: 15 global skills are vendored/symlinked from `mattpocock/skills` upstream, they *pull our project structure toward an upstream ecosystem's expected layout* (`CONTEXT.md`, `docs/agents/`, issue-tracker assumptions), and `/tdd` is already a **divergent project-local fork** with "two copies, no sync" [map §1 "CONTEXT.md coupling"; §3b global skills; disk `.claude/skills/tdd/SKILL.md`]. That is an owner-drift + fork-maintenance cost the map *inventories* but never *prices as a risk* the way it prices, say, the enforcement gap. **Gap: V2 has no stated policy for the upstream-skill dependency** — vendor-and-freeze, track-upstream, or cut. This is the article's most transferable single insight, and it lands on a real, already-documented coupling.

2. **The "self-host vs. cloud, priced in labor" frame exposes that our map prices build-cost but not ongoing-maintenance-cost per surviving item.** The article's sharpest correction (Mistake 3) is that the headline acquisition cost hides the *recurring* maintenance cost; n8n self-host "looks free, costs $300–750/mo." Our build-or-reject registries [map §5 canon-only, §6 disk-only] decide *build / document / delete* but carry **no maintenance-cost column** — yet several §5 items (`branch-registry-guard.sh` + `active-branches.json`, pre-push auto-rebase sync gate, `session-end.sh` memory-candidate hook) are exactly the kind of stateful, drift-prone machinery that is cheap to write and expensive to keep correct. **Gap: §5/§6 dispositions lack a recurring-cost lens.** The article supplies the missing column: for each surviving mechanism, name its ongoing maintenance failure mode (the §9 golden rule applied to *upkeep*, not just *existence*).

3. **"Durability as the missing primitive" (pass-2 §5) maps directly to our confirmed enforcement absence — and reframes it.** The article's durable-execution insight: the convenience tier is fragile precisely because *state is not persisted across a crash mid-run.* Our harness is overwhelmingly **advisory**: "neither [canon nor disk] has a deterministic backstop for the bulk of skill bodies, CLAUDE.md rules, or the autoMode lists" [map §3e net-enforcement; §0]. The pipeline's own continuity depends on sentinels (`.cr-ok`) that are gitignored and **never reach CI** [map §3f, the "Node 8.5(c) gap"]. The article's frame says: an advisory system has no checkpoint — if an agent run is interrupted between "skill says do X" and "X done," nothing resumes or verifies it. **Gap (already a confirmed absence, now reframed):** the missing structural floor (§3e — `block-dangerous-bash.sh`, `enforce-scope.sh`, branch-registry guard all ABSENT on disk; sentinel never verified in CI §3f) *is* our durability gap. The article doesn't add a new gap; it gives a sharper name for one §3e/§3f already confirms, which strengthens the case for the deterministic backstop V2 is weighing.

(No fourth gap. MCP-as-substrate (pass-2 §4) is real for the *tool market* but does not map to a harness row — our harness *is* the MCP client, it does not need to expose connectors. I refuse to invent a gap there.)

## (c) The article's own weaknesses (and why most of it doesn't apply to us)

- **Domain mismatch is near-total.** This is the dominant weakness *for our purposes*: the article is about connecting SaaS apps and running durable business workflows. Our harness orchestrates an LLM coding agent. The taxonomy (Cat 1–5), every pricing table, and every tool verdict are inert here. Anyone citing "n8n" or "Temporal" as a V2 build item would be making the exact category error the article itself condemns (Mistake 1) — comparing across categories that don't belong together. Guard against it.
- **The catalog rots faster than the method.** Pass-2 §6 flagged the internal contradictions; for *application* the relevant rot is temporal: Pipedream's independence is already gone, Airflow 2.x is already EOL, license terms and pricing shift quarterly. Only the four disciplines survive — and (a) shows we already hold them. The half-life of this article's *facts* is months; its *method* we already had.
- **Uneven skepticism (pass-2 §6.5).** It nukes vendor ROI surveys (Mistake 7) but credulously repeats "13x faster" (Windmill self-benchmark), "8–10 hrs/week/rep" (Bardeen), "$310/mo saved" (one case). For us this is a *cautionary mirror*, not a flaw to fix: it's the same trap as citing our own un-benchmarked harness claims (e.g. "operates at 60% of model capacity" [canon §0/§9] — a vendor-of-self number we should treat with the skepticism the article failed to apply to Windmill).
- **Taxonomy leaks at every tool that matters (pass-2 §2).** The single-category framing breaks on n8n/Windmill/Pipedream, which span categories. The transferable warning: rigid registers (our §5/§6/§4/§9 buckets) can mis-file the items that span them — e.g. the prod-key firewall is simultaneously a disk-only advance (§6) *and* part of the structural floor (§3e). Don't let the register hide a spanning item.
- **The budget matrix contradicts the prose (pass-2 §6.2)** — "$0 self-hosted" reintroduces the free-lunch illusion the n8n section just demolished. Cautionary mirror for our own §5: don't let a summary table re-list a "free" mechanism whose real cost we just argued is recurring (gap b.2).

## (d) Is fresh research warranted? — No. Synthesize.

**Recommendation: synthesize, do not commission new research.** Reasons, against the standing "prefer synthesize" bias:

- **The transferable content is already ours.** (a) shows all four disciplines are already encoded in canon §9, the reversibility doctrine, and the map's citation rule. There is nothing to *learn* — only to *apply* to three rows already in the map.
- **The new-signal items (b.1, b.2, b.3) are synthesis tasks, not research tasks.** b.1 (price the upstream-skill owner-drift risk) needs a *decision* on an already-inventoried coupling [map §1, §3b], not new external research. b.2 (add a maintenance-cost lens to §5/§6) is an internal re-pass of the registries. b.3 (durability = our enforcement floor) is reframing a confirmed absence [§3e/§3f], not finding a new one.
- **Fresh research here would re-import domain-mismatched facts** — the exact risk in (c). Commissioning "evaluate n8n/Temporal for the harness" would burn budget producing the category error the article warns against.
- **One narrow exception, low priority:** *if* V2 ever considers exposing harness skills as MCP tools to other agents (the pass-2 §4 substrate shift), the Pipedream/Composio/n8n MCP-server patterns become directly relevant prior art — but that is speculative scope (To-Think-About territory), not a current gap, and building toward it now would violate "build what's needed now." Note it; do not research it yet.

**Net for the V2 audit:** fold three things into the existing map — (1) a one-line *upstream-dependency policy* gap against §1/§3b (vendor-freeze vs. track vs. cut the Matt-Pocock coupling, the `/tdd` fork being the live cost), (2) a *recurring-maintenance-cost column* on the §5/§6 build-or-reject registries (the article's strongest reusable correction), and (3) the durability framing as a sharper *name* for the §3e/§3f advisory-floor absence. Everything else in the article is a well-written buyer's guide for a market we are not in.
