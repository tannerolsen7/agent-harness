# Pass 3 — Apply: the Harness.io article against OUR harness

Building on pass2: the article reduces to one transferable principle (pass2 §A — *context files are non-deterministic behavior-influencing inputs, audit them as such*), an ownership claim that collapses to "colocate the spec" at solo scale (pass2 §C), an audit criterion that would delete dormant safety rules unless guarded (pass2 §D), and urgency borrowed from unverified stats (pass2 §E). I now map that residue onto the ground-truth map (`CANONICAL-HARNESS-AS-IS.md`). Every gap cites a ground-truth section or a confirmed absence.

## (a) What we ALREADY do

- **".claude/ as the governance layer" — already true, and richer than the article's solo-equivalent.** The article (pass1 §6) names ".claude/ as the governance layer; compound agent contract as policy" as our equivalent of Harness's platform. Ground truth confirms this exists and exceeds the sketch: `[disk §3a]` lists `.claude/SOUL.md`, `agent-contract.md`, `INDEX.md`, `AI-WORKFLOW.md`, and `[disk §3e]` shows real PreToolUse enforcement hooks. The article's "principle fully [transfers]" is not a gap — it's a description of our status quo.
- **"Claude Code permission model + PreToolUse hooks" as the RBAC equivalent — already built.** `[disk §3e]` documents `block-dangerous-git.sh`, `block-npm-install.sh`, `permission-logger.sh`, plus the `worktree-create.sh` prod-key firewall (Tier-0 credential isolation, a disk *advance* over canon). The article's "principle yes; mechanism different" maps onto mechanisms we already run.
- **In-pipeline verification at our scale — already built.** The article concedes the solo equivalent is "agent runs in GitHub Actions." `[disk §3f]` confirms `ci.yml` + `integration.yml` run tsc/lint/vitest on every PR. We already do agent-output-validated-in-pipeline; we just don't (and shouldn't) do Harness's agent-*inside*-the-pipeline-stage.
- **A precision standard for context files already exists in canon.** `[canon §9]` (Model Capacity Audit) already encodes the article's "every line must earn its place" instinct as the golden rule: *"If you can't name a failure mode the constraint prevents, the constraint is overhead."* The article's Design Challenge is a **rediscovery** of a discipline we have already written down — and, as pass2 §D shows, our version is **safer** (it protects dormant safety rules; the article's "does it change behavior?" test does not).
- **Single source of truth for skills — already the model.** Article names "Notion as skill source of truth; SKILL.md files as enforcement." `[canon §1]` (Notion canon) + `[disk §3b]` (26 skill dirs) confirm exactly this two-layer arrangement already exists (with its own drift problems, but the *pattern* is present).

## (b) REAL gaps the article exposes (each citation-backed)

1. **No line-level execution-surface vs documentation classification of CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md exists — and our own canon already flags AGENTS.md as carrying dead/contradictory content.** `[canon §3a]` records that event-vendor's `AGENTS.md` "has the 'None open' contradiction" and `[canon §7 item 9]` records an "un-applied 'add these rows manually' block (self-declared mid-migration)" living in the file. These are precisely *documentation-shaped cruft that no longer changes behavior* — the article's target class. The gap is real: **no pass, skill, or hook currently classifies CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md entries by whether they change agent behavior.** This is a genuine method we lack, citable to the contradictions §7 already catalogs.

2. **The classification, if adopted, has no home — `/scan-context` does NOT exist on disk.** The article proposes running the audit "when running /scan-context." Confirmed absence: `[canon §5]` lists `/scan-context` as **"Documented in canon, ABSENT on disk"**, and direct disk check this session found no `.claude/skills/scan-context` dir. So the article's proposed *mechanism* lands on a skill we never built. The gap is therefore **two-layered**: (i) we lack the classification method, and (ii) we lack the `/scan-context` surface the article assumes would host it. Either we build `/scan-context` (a `[canon §5]` build-or-reject decision) or attach the audit to `/cr`/`/compound` (which exist, `[disk §3b]`).

3. **The article's "AgentLint check" does not exist in our system — a false premise to correct, not a gap to fill.** The article asserts the line-level audit "is already implied by the AgentLint check ('only write what agents can't discover themselves')." Direct disk check found **no AgentLint** anywhere in `.claude/` or `docs/`, and it appears nowhere in the ground-truth map. This is the article importing a mechanism from *another* research page as if it were ours. Not a gap in our harness — a **provenance error in the article** (also logged in (c)). The underlying rule ("don't write what agents can discover") is worth having, but we'd be *originating* it, not inheriting it.

4. **No feature-area-scoped context files — confirmed, and the article's open question is now answered.** The article ends unable to answer "does event-vendor have feature-area-scoped AGENTS.md files, or is everything in one root file?" Direct disk check this session: the **only** `AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md` are at repo root (the eight worktree copies are full clones of root, not module-scoped sections). So: **everything is in one root file.** Whether this is a *gap* is genuinely open — pass2 §C shows the ownership argument collapses to "colocate the spec" at solo scale, and `[canon §3a]` already treats root CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md as "aligned in purpose." Recommendation: **not a gap worth closing now.** Splitting into feature-area files at solo scale buys discovery-locality the article can't show matters (its discovery hierarchy is unverified, pass2 §E) at the cost of more files to keep non-stale — and `[canon §4]` already shows our staleness/duplication problem is our worst one. File under §6-style "reject with reason," not build.

5. **The duplication problem the article would *worsen* is one our canon already names.** The article's disposal target is "move documentation lines to CONTEXT.md." But `[canon §4]` documents a **triple-duplication** (memory.md + RECURRING-FINDINGS + PITFALLS + auto-memory) the canon "both sanctions and forbids," and `[canon §3a]` calls CONTEXT.md "the *why*, the hardest doc." Relocating CLAUDE.md prose into CONTEXT.md without a one-writer/one-reader rule (the §4 Phase-3 target) just moves staleness into the hardest-to-maintain file. The real gap the article *points at but mis-prescribes*: **we have no single owner/reader/freshness rule per knowledge store** (`[canon §4]` Phase-3 crux). Adopt the article's *audit*, but route disposal through the §4 model, not a naive "dump into CONTEXT.md."

## (c) Weaknesses in the article's OWN reasoning

- **"Execution surface" overclaims determinism** (pass2 §B): the metaphor borrows compiler-grade reliability for a probabilistic reader. "Does this line change agent behavior?" is *decidable* for a config flag, *empirical and eval-requiring* for an LLM — the article treats it as eyeball-inspectable. Without an eval harness (which the article never mentions), the audit is judgment, not measurement.
- **The audit criterion would delete the safety floor** (pass2 §D): Harness asks "does it change behavior?"; dormant safety rules (destructive-op rules, no-token-reuse, no-push-without-/cr) change behavior in ~0% of sessions and would score as removable. Our canon's criterion — `[canon §9]` "name the failure mode it prevents" — is strictly safer. **Do not import the Harness phrasing; keep ours.**
- **Urgency is borrowed from disclaimed numbers** (pass2 §E): the "50% never-modified" stat and the 100/80/40/<10 discovery hierarchy are flagged *by the article itself* as Harness-sourced and unverified. Remove them and the principle survives but the urgency deflates to "some files go stale." Don't let an unverified stat set our priority.
- **Ownership ≠ authorship** (pass2 §C): the ETH Zurich bridge measures *human-authored* beating *auto-generated*; the article reuses it to justify *module-team ownership*. Different claim. At solo scale only the authorship point survives, and we already author by hand.
- **Two failure modes fused** (pass2 §F): "consistency collapse" (ungoverned AGENTS.md) and the Ramp pivot (too many specialist agents) are different phenomena merged to co-confirm a governance sell. Our `[canon §3d]` 23-agent roster is governed centrally already; the Ramp lesson, if anything, is "don't proliferate agents," which is orthogonal to this article's AGENTS.md thesis.
- **Self-inconsistent application** (pass2 §H): the article wants every line to be precision execution-surface *and* wants non-specialist module owners editing it casually during feature work. Precision instruments aren't maintained as afterthoughts. The solo translation ("add a line to /compound") drops the review Harness's platform supplies.

## (d) Does it warrant fresh external research?

**No.** Disciplined synthesize-over-research call, for three reasons:

1. The one transferable principle is already a **rediscovery of `[canon §9]`** — we don't need an external source to validate a discipline our own canon states more safely.
2. The article's only novel empirical content (the 50% stat, the discovery hierarchy) is **self-disclaimed as unverified Harness marketing** (pass2 §E). Re-researching to confirm a vendor stat we'd discard anyway is low-value.
3. Parts 2–5 of the Harness series are unread (pass1 §0, the article's own open question #2). Tempting, but the through-line is a CI/CD-vendor governance pitch the article already conceded "is overkill for a solo developer on Vercel + Supabase + GitHub." Reading four more vendor posts to extract more non-transferable product detail fails the cost/benefit bar.

**One narrow exception, only if a future phase greenlights it:** the *eval-harness* question raised in pass2 §B — *how do you actually measure whether a CLAUDE.md line changes agent behavior?* — is a real open problem this article exposes but doesn't solve. If V2 decides the execution-surface audit must be measured rather than eyeballed, that is worth fresh research (LLM-behavior-eval / context-ablation methods), and it would be **new** to our corpus. Until then: synthesize, don't research.
