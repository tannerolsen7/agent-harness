# Pass 3 — Apply: CodeRabbit against OUR harness (the ground-truth map)

Building on pass2: the operative thesis is not "AI reads code" but "review is the conserved scarcity, and CodeRabbit is a *buffer* that absorbs review volume so the human gate doesn't become the ceiling" (pass2 §A). Pass2 §C reframed its limitation from *PR size* to *memory locality* — it cannot see anything whose correctness depends on durable project memory the diff doesn't contain. Pass2 §F named the unstated assumption: the article is GitHub-review-centric and never asks what an AI reviewer adds *on top of deterministic pre-PR enforcement*. Those three are the lenses for applying this to our harness.

## (a) What we ALREADY do

Our harness's answer to "review is the bottleneck" is not a SaaS reviewer — it is an **in-harness multi-pass review pipeline**, and it is more governance-aware than CodeRabbit by construction:

- **`/cr` — 9-pass + adversarial review against the full branch diff.** [canon §3c; disk `HARNESS-AS-IS §4`: "9 parallel passes (1–9) + Pass 11 `@reviewer` (4 lenses)"]. This is our standing first-pass reviewer. Unlike CodeRabbit's single semantic pass, it runs assumption/composition/cascade/abuse lenses [§3d: "all 4 lenses (assumption/composition/cascade/abuse)"]. It addresses pass2 §A's "buffer before the human gate" directly — it runs before push, auto-fixes MUST-FIX, and writes the `.cr-ok` sentinel.
- **`/cr-security` — dedicated security/auth/data-boundary review** (disk: 3 passes) [§3c, §6]. Covers the "false-positive-rate on security findings" worry the article raises as an open question — except ours is INVOKER-aware and tenant-isolation-aware, not generic.
- **Deterministic pre-PR enforcement floor** — the thing pass2 §F says the article never contemplates. We have: `block-dangerous-git.sh`, `block-npm-install.sh` (exit 2) [§3e]; pre-commit running ESLint + `tsc --noEmit` + vitest [CLAUDE.md workflow step 2]; pre-push validating the `.cr-ok` sentinel + integration tests + `next build` [§3e pre-push row]; CI re-running tsc/lint/vitest [§3f]. CodeRabbit's "first-pass gate" sits where we already have a *blocking* gate.
- **Recurring-findings counter** — `RECURRING-FINDINGS.md`, auto-counted by `/cr` Step 3b [canon §4]. This is the article's Candidate-2 ("aggregate findings → PITFALLS") *already partially built*, and ours closes more of the loop than the article's manual "promote top 3."
- **Knowledge promotion lifecycle** — `memory.md` → `RECURRING-FINDINGS` → (human-confirmed) → `PITFALLS.md` [canon §4]. The evidence-based-PITFALLS idea the article proposes as new is our existing promotion path.
- **Verification before done** — we have the `/verify` skill and integration tests against a real Supabase instance [CLAUDE.md testing; disk skills]. This is our local equivalent of the article's "Ramp verifies / Basis diff-scoped tests" pipeline stage (pass1 cross-page synthesis).

Net: the article's whole proposal ("add an AI first-pass reviewer before the human") describes a capability we run *in-process* via `/cr`, with deterministic gates the article's studied teams apparently lack.

## (b) REAL gaps it exposes — each cited to the map

1. **No PR-surface review at all; `/cr` is pre-push and self-reported.** Our review runs locally before push and gates on the `.cr-ok` sentinel — but [§3f] records the **Node 8.5(c) gap: "CI never verifies `.cr-ok`" … "gitignored, never reaches CI"]. So the review is real but its *artifact never reaches the PR or CI*. CodeRabbit's structural advantage is that review output lands **on the PR, in CI's view, auditable** — exactly the surface our sentinel can't reach. The gap is not "we lack a reviewer"; it is **the reviewer's verdict is invisible at the PR/CI boundary** [§3f, confirmed disk fact].

2. **`/cr` review quality is never independently calibrated.** Pass2 §E split "available" from "good"; pass2 §D showed the article can't verify "semantic." The same blade cuts us: nothing in the map records a *measurement* of `/cr`'s precision/recall. [§3c] shows three sources disagree on even the *pass count* ("9 plus adversarial" vs "9 including adversarial," `/cr-security` 2 vs 3) — if we can't pin the structure, we certainly haven't measured the output quality. Confirmed absence: no calibration/benchmark of `/cr` findings anywhere in §3–§9 (the only benchmark reference, `@benchmark-runner`, is a §6 phantom — "Referenced on disk, never built"). The article's Design Challenge (run it on a real PR; answer what it caught/missed) is the calibration protocol we have never run on our *own* reviewer.

3. **Our reviewer's governance-awareness is asserted, not wired.** Pass2 §C: the failures that matter are governance-global (locked ADRs, Rejected Patterns, PITFALLS). [§5] lists `docs/adr/` decisions as a knowledge store; CLAUDE.md names Rejected Patterns and role-in-RLS as a hard "explicitly rejected" rule. But there is no confirmed mechanism in the map that *feeds the ADR/Rejected-Patterns/PITFALLS corpus into the `/cr` passes as review criteria* — `RECURRING-FINDINGS` is counted, but the locked-decision corpus is not demonstrably an input. Confirmed absence: no §3c/§4 row wiring ADR/Rejected-Patterns into review. This is the one place CodeRabbit's limitation (pass2 §C) is *also our latent limitation* unless explicitly closed.

4. **No cross-repo / multi-project review reality.** Pass1's code-graph-for-cross-repo "doesn't transfer yet." Confirmed by [§8 Cross-project reality]: "the harness has never been installed anywhere but event-vendor"; recyclops + logistics-service carry no harness. So any reviewer (ours or CodeRabbit's) that would reason across repos has *no multi-repo substrate to reason over* — the gap is upstream of review entirely [§8, §0 "single-project artifact"].

## (c) Weaknesses in the article's OWN reasoning

- **Verifies the unverifiable.** Pass2 §D: "semantic diffs" is marked **Verified** while the page's own Open Question #2 admits it doesn't know what "semantic" means, and its research-limits note says to treat vendor claims as vendor claims. The capability claims (the decision-relevant ones) are vendor-sourced and soft; only the scale numbers are triangulated.
- **Conflates availability with quality.** Pass2 §E: "structural improvement, not a productivity feature" is true only for *availability*; "finds real defects" is asserted, and the page itself flags false-positive rate as unknown. The strongest-sounding claim is half-supported.
- **Under-weights its own best fact.** Pass2 §A: the 1.7x-more-comments-on-AI-PRs figure is the page's most consequential evidence (review cost rises super-linearly with agent output) yet sits as one ledger row, not the thesis.
- **Solo-scale verdicts are unearned.** Pass2 §B: the "fully transfers" calls in the Doesn't-Transfer table rest on an unmeasured precision assumption; at solo scale the tool competes for the *same* attention it claims to save, and the article never nets that out.
- **GitHub-review-centric blind spot.** Pass2 §F: the article never asks what an AI reviewer adds *on top of* deterministic pre-commit/pre-push gates — because the teams it studies seem to use review *as* the gate. For a harness that already blocks categorically at commit/push, that is the only question that matters, and it's absent.
- **Leaves the compounding loop half-open.** Pass2 §G: Candidate-2 stops at "manually promote top 3"; it never closes finding → rule → enforcement → fewer findings, which is what would make the analytics more than a dashboard.

## (d) Does it warrant fresh external research? — Mostly no; one narrow yes

Disciplined verdict: **synthesize, don't re-research**, with a single scoped exception.

- **No re-research needed** for the thesis (review is the bottleneck) — already confirmed cross-company in our own corpus (pass1 cross-page; the AI-Native research tree [memory: reference_ai_native_engineering_research]) and it maps cleanly to gap (b)(1)–(b)(2). Re-reading more reviewer-vendor pages would re-confirm a settled point.
- **No re-research needed** for "should we adopt CodeRabbit specifically." The article's *own* Design Challenge is an empirical test, not a literature question — the answer comes from running a tool on one PR (gap b1's surface problem), not from more reading. If we want it, that's an experiment, not research.
- **One narrow YES:** gap (b)(2) — *how to calibrate `/cr`'s own precision/recall* — is a genuine open methodology question, and it is internal, not about CodeRabbit. The fresh input worth seeking is **review-tool evaluation methodology** (how teams measure AI-reviewer false-positive/false-negative rates against a labeled defect set), because [§6] confirms our `@benchmark-runner` is a phantom and we have no measurement harness for our reviewer. That is a real, citable absence — and the only thread here that a short external pass would actually move.
