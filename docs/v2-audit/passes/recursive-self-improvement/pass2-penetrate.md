# Pass 2 — Penetrate

Building on pass1: the article's surface argument (P1 §"core question") is "verification is the scarce capability." That is the *stated* thesis. The deeper move — and the reason this page is unusual in the batch — is structural, not topical. Below: the real thesis under the stated one, the load-bearing assumption it never defends, the contradictions it half-sees, and net-new analysis the article does not itself make.

## The deeper thesis: a *forgeability* argument wearing a *scarcity* argument's clothes

Building on pass1's three pillars (P1 §"verified pillars") and the four findings (P1 §"application"), the article *says* verification is scarce. But every concrete recommendation is actually about one narrower property: **whether a stop condition can be forged by the thing it is supposed to gate.** Re-read P1's Finding 1 — the complaint is not that verification is hard, it is that `MUST-FIX=0` is *self-certified*: the generator's own model family computes the gate that releases the generator's output. Finding 2 is the same shape one level up: the gate `/cr` (the verifier) is itself ungated — its recall is self-asserted, never measured against an authority it can't move. Finding 3 (PBT) is the same shape again: a property is valuable *precisely because* "the model can't argue with it" (P1). Finding 4 (no paying stranger) is the same shape at the outermost ring: the whole system's fitness is self-certified against a friendly audience.

So the real thesis is sharper than "verification is scarce": **every quality gate in an automated loop degenerates into a measure of the model's self-agreement unless its authority is anchored to something the model cannot redefine.** Scarcity is the symptom; *forgeability of the stop authority* is the disease. This reframing matters because it changes what "fixing it" means — not "verify more" (you can verify infinitely and still be inside the loop), but "move the *terminal* authority outside the loop." The article gestures at this with "cross-AUTHORITY not cross-MODEL" (P1 Finding 1) but does not name the general principle. I name it: **authority laundering** — the loop converts a model's preference into an apparent objective signal by passing it through enough review passes that it *looks* external while remaining model-internal.

## The single load-bearing assumption the article never defends

Building on pass1's Pillar 1 (P1: "software is unusually amenable to oversight because outputs can be executed, compared, tested"): the entire argument rests on **the existence of a cheap, trustworthy oracle** (CI / property test). The article treats the oracle as *given* and *clean*. That is the unexamined assumption. Two cracks it never addresses:

1. **The oracle is itself authored — increasingly by the same model.** A CI test, a property, an invariant — these are code. If >80% of merged code is model-authored (P1 §"what the source says"), then over time the *oracle* is model-authored too. A model that writes both the implementation and the property that certifies it has re-internalized the loop one level down. The article's own headline statistic quietly undermines its own proposed cure, and it does not notice. The defense exists — a human specifies the *invariant* even if the model writes the test harness (P1 Finding 3, "human-specified invariant") — but the article never confronts that the human-specification step is the actual scarce resource, not "verification" in the abstract. **The scarce capability is not verification; it is the human-authored, model-immutable specification of *what correct means*.** Everything else (CI, PBT, golden set) is machinery for *enforcing* a spec; none of it *produces* the spec.

2. **An oracle only certifies what it is pointed at.** CI-green means "the checks that exist passed," not "the code is correct." Routing the stop rule through CI (P1 Finding 1) upgrades the gate from forgeable to unforgeable *only over the checks that exist* — and the set of checks is, again, model-influenced and incomplete. So "MUST-FIX=0 AND CI-green" is strictly better than "MUST-FIX=0 alone," but it is not the clean external authority the article's prose implies. It is a *less forgeable* gate, not an *unforgeable* one. The article oversells the cure by treating CI-green as a truth oracle when it is a coverage-bounded oracle.

## A contradiction the article half-sees and does not resolve

Building on pass1's Finding 2 (golden set) vs Finding 1 (CI gate): these two cures are in latent tension and the article orders them without noticing the tension.

- Finding 1 says the *terminal* authority is CI + human. CI is a hard oracle.
- Finding 2 says calibrate `/cr` with a **golden set** — a set of known-defective diffs with known-correct findings, recall measured.

But a golden set is **a model-style verifier's report card, graded by a human-curated key.** It calibrates the *soft* reviewer (`/cr`), which Finding 1 just demoted below the *hard* oracle (CI). So the article spends Finding 1 arguing "stop trusting the soft model gate, route through the hard oracle," then spends Finding 2 arguing "invest heavily in calibrating the soft model gate." Both can be true — calibrate the soft gate *and* subordinate it to the hard gate — but the article never states the relationship. The resolution it omits: **the golden set's real job is not to make `/cr` trustworthy as a stop authority; it is to tell you how much un-trapped defect mass `/cr` lets through *so you can size the human/CI net beneath it.*** A calibrated soft gate is a *triage* layer that reduces human load, never a *terminal* authority. The article conflates "calibrated" with "promotable to authority." It is not. Calibration tells you the soft gate's leakage rate; it does not make leakage zero.

## What the article takes for granted about *our* system (net-new)

Building on pass1's Findings 1-2, the article asserts "MUST-FIX=0 is computed by model reviewers, not the oracle" and "`/cr` catch-rate is unmeasured." It takes for granted that **these are diagnoses of a coherent verifier that merely lacks calibration.** Net-new observation it does not make: the more damaging reading is that **there is no single object called "the verifier" to calibrate.** `/cr` is N parallel model passes whose outputs are *merged by an aggregation rule* into MUST-FIX/NEEDS-HUMAN/SUGGESTION tiers. Recall is not a property of `/cr`; it is a property of (the passes) × (the merge rule) × (the model version) × (the diff distribution). A golden set measured today on Opus 4.8 calibrates a moving object — change the model, the passes, or the tier-merge logic and the number is stale. The article's "build the golden set" is necessary but it quietly assumes calibration is a one-time act. **It is a continuous-integration concern: recall must be re-measured on every change to the reviewer, or the calibration itself becomes a forgeable, stale gate — the exact failure mode the article is warning about, reproduced inside the cure.** This is the article's own thesis turned back on its own remedy, and it does not perform that turn.

## Where the article's discipline is genuinely strong (so Pass 3 inherits it)

Building on pass1 §"what to discount": the article models the behavior it preaches. It *self-discounts* its own strongest-sounding statistic (80% authorship ≠ autonomy), flags its own paraphrase ("18-20% ceiling is approximate; citable evidence is 16.6% vs 56.5%"), and separates the technical core from the strategic wrapper. This is not decoration — it is the cheapest available demonstration of "the maker stating its own confidence bounds." Pass 3 should treat the article's *flagged* numbers as soft and its *cited-with-arXiv* numbers (self-preference ~10%, adoption 16.6/56.5, >50% wrong) as the load-bearing facts. The article has, in effect, pre-labeled which of its own claims are oracle-grade and which are rhetoric. That is rare and should be honored.

## Distilled thesis carried into Pass 3

The scarce capability is not verification — it is **a human-authored, model-immutable specification of correctness, plus a terminal stop authority anchored to it.** A verification stack without that anchor is authority laundering: it converts model self-agreement into an apparent objective signal. The three cures (CI-gated stop, golden-set calibration, property tests) are valuable in exactly that order *only if* (a) the golden set is treated as continuous calibration of a *triage* layer, not a one-time blessing of an *authority* layer, and (b) someone accepts that the spec itself — not its enforcement — is the true bottleneck.
